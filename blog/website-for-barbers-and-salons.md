# What a Barber or Salon Website Needs (and What It Doesn't)

A short checklist for barbershop, salon and beauty websites: prices, hours, booking on WhatsApp, and the extras you can safely skip.

A barbershop or salon website has a small job, and that is good news. Nobody reads a salon's history. They want to see what you do, what it costs, when you are open and how to get a slot. If your site does those four things well on a phone, it is doing its job.

Most of your clients will find you on Instagram, through a friend, or by walking past. The website is where they go to check the details before they message you. Build it for that moment.

## The checklist

### 1. A price list, with real prices

This is the most useful thing on the page. "Signature Fade, NGN 3,000" answers the question that otherwise arrives as a DM, fifty times a week.

- List your main services with a price each.
- Use "from" if the price depends on hair length or style.
- Group them if you serve different clients. A unisex salon can split the list into "For Her" and "For Him".
- Keep it to the services people actually book. Ten clear items beat thirty.

### 2. Hours that tell the truth

Opening days and times, plainly. Then go one step further: tell people when it is quiet and when it is busy. Something like "Mornings are walk-ins only, evenings get busy so book ahead" helps clients choose a time and spreads your work across the day.

### 3. One booking button

"Book on WhatsApp" at the top and again at the bottom. It opens a chat with your number so the client can ask for a slot in their own words. That is all most shops need.

### 4. Where you are

The address, a landmark and a map link. If you are upstairs or behind another building, say so.

### 5. Answers to the questions you hear every day

Three or four short ones are enough:

- Do I need an appointment, or can I walk in?
- How long does a cut, a set of lashes or a braiding style take?
- What payment do you take, cash or transfer?
- Do you do kids' cuts or home service?

### 6. Something that changes

A "cut of the week" or "style of the week" block with a photo, a price and a line about it. It gives regular clients a reason to look again, and it gives you an easy thing to update.

## What you can skip

- **An About page with your life story.** One line about the shop is plenty.
- **A blog.** Your Instagram already shows your work.
- **A full online booking system.** Unless you have several chairs and a lot of no-shows, WhatsApp does the job. A system that checks live availability, takes deposits and sends reminders needs a database, and the [pricing page](https://elevven11studio.github.io/pricing/) lists booking systems as quoted separately.
- **Taking payments on the site.** Card payments online are also quoted separately. Most shops are fine taking cash or transfer in the chair.

## Common mistakes

- **Prices from two years ago.** If a client arrives expecting the old price, you start the visit with an argument.
- **Photos that are not yours.** Stock photos of models make people wonder what you are hiding. Use clear photos of your own work and your own shop.
- **A link-only Instagram bio.** If your Instagram bio links to a page with no prices and no hours, you have sent the client in a circle.
