# What a Restaurant Website Needs: Menu, Hours and Orders

How to set up a restaurant, kitchen or cafe website around your menu, opening hours and orders, and when a simple site is enough versus a store.

Hungry people are impatient readers. Someone looking at your restaurant website is usually deciding between you and two other places in the next few minutes. They want to see the food, the prices, whether you are open, and how to order or book. Anything else gets skipped.

The good news is that a restaurant website can be simple. The hard part is keeping it accurate.

## Decide what the site is for

Before you think about design, be clear about the main thing you want people to do. It changes the layout.

| Your business | Main action | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Home-style kitchen or buka | Order for pickup or delivery | Popular dishes and an "Order on WhatsApp" button |
| Cafe or bakery | Visit or order ahead | Favourites, hours and location |
| Sit-down restaurant or lounge | Reserve a table | Chef's picks and a "Reserve" button |
| Caterer or food vendor | Request a quote for an event | Past events, menus and a contact button |

One main action, repeated at the top and bottom of the page, works better than five competing buttons.

## The menu question

Do you need your full menu online? Usually not. A list of your most popular dishes with prices does more work than forty items nobody reads on a phone. Show six to ten dishes people actually order, each with a short description and a price.

If your menu changes daily, add a "today's special" block instead of trying to keep the whole menu up to date. It gives regulars a reason to check back, and it is quick to change.

Whatever you show, keep the prices current. A customer who orders from an old price and gets charged more is unlikely to order again.

## Details customers look for

- **Opening hours by day**, and kitchen hours by meal if they differ. "Breakfast 7 to 10:30, grills from 6pm" tells people when to come for what.
- **A full address with a landmark** and a map link.
- **Delivery area and fees.** Even a rough rule, such as "we confirm the fee before you pay", saves back and forth.
- **Event and bulk orders.** If you cater, say how much notice you need.
- **Dietary options.** A line about vegetarian or pepper-free dishes answers a common question.

## Mistakes that cost orders

1. **Photos that do not match the plate.** Use clear photos of your own food. Stock photos set up expectations you cannot meet.
2. **A PDF menu.** It is slow to open on a phone and hard to read. Put dishes on the page as text.
3. **Old hours.** If you close early on Mondays, the site needs to say so.
4. **No obvious way to order.** If people have to hunt for a phone number, they will order from the place that made it easy.

## Simple site or online store?

This is the real decision for a food business.

A **simple site** shows your dishes, hours and location, with a WhatsApp button. The customer types their order into the chat. This suits kitchens that take a modest number of orders, or where the menu changes too often to list.

An **online store** lets customers pick dishes, add them to a cart and send a finished order to your WhatsApp. Our Store package includes a cart that remembers items, WhatsApp checkout with the total and delivery fee, delivery zones, pickup and a minimum order. That suits kitchens with a fixed menu and steady delivery orders, because orders arrive complete and priced.

Neither option takes card payments on the site. Online payments are quoted separately on the [pricing page](https://elevven11studio.github.io/pricing/). ## Keeping the menu honest

Price and dish changes count as free updates during your update window: 2 weeks on Starter, 1 month on Plus and Store. Store updates include prices and stock. After that, plan how often you will send changes.
