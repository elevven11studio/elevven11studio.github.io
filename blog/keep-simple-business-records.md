# How to Keep Simple Business Records

The five records every small business should keep, how to keep them on paper or in an app, and a short daily and monthly routine that works.

Good records are not about paperwork for its own sake. They answer the questions you ask every week: Am I making money? What is selling? Who owes me? Where did the cash go?

Without them, you are guessing. With a few simple habits, you know.

## The five records to keep

Most small businesses need only these.

### Sales

Every sale, with the date, what was sold, the amount and how it was paid: cash, transfer, POS or credit. Daily totals are the minimum. Item by item is better, because it tells you what actually sells.

### Expenses

Everything the business pays for: stock, rent, transport, fuel, data, repairs, salaries. Write down the date, amount, what it was for and who you paid. Small daily costs matter most, because they are the ones people forget.

### Stock

What you have, what came in and what went out. You need this to reorder on time, spot theft or waste and know what your shelves are worth. A regular count keeps the record honest. See [how to do a stock count](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/how-to-do-a-stock-count/).

### Invoices and credit

Who owes you, how much and since when. And the other side: what you owe suppliers. If you sell on credit, this record is how you get paid. Our guide on [how to sell on credit without losing money](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/track-customer-credit-and-debts/) explains how to keep it.

### Receipts

Proof of money in and out. Keep supplier receipts for what you bought and copies of receipts you give customers. They settle disputes and back up your expense record.

## Paper or app?

Both work. The best system is the one you will actually use every day.

| | Paper | App |
|---|---|---|
| Cost to start | A notebook and a pen | Free or paid, plus a device |
| Learning | None | A short setup |
| Totals and reports | You add up by hand | Worked out for you |
| Mistakes | Easy to miss | Fewer adding errors |
| Losing it | Fire, water, a missing page | Needs backups |
| Searching old entries | Slow | Quick |

### Keeping records on paper

If you use paper, keep it organised:

- Use separate notebooks, or separate sections, for sales, expenses, stock and debts.
- Write in pen, not pencil, and do not tear out pages. Cross out mistakes with one line.
- Add up each day and each month on the same line every time.
- Keep receipts in an envelope or file for each month.

For tips on storing papers so you can find them later, read [how to organize paper files in your office](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/organize-paper-files-in-your-office/).

## A simple routine

Records only work if you keep them up. This routine takes a few minutes a day.

**Every day**

1. Record every sale and expense as it happens, not from memory at night.
2. Count the cash at closing and compare it with recorded cash sales.
3. File today's receipts.

**Every week**

1. Check who owes you and follow up on anything due.
2. Look at low stock and plan your next order.

**Every month**

1. Total sales and expenses.
2. Work out profit: sales, minus the cost of what you sold, minus expenses.
3. Count stock, or at least your fast-moving items.
4. Back up your records. Photograph notebook pages, or export from your app.

## Keep business money out of personal records

Records only make sense if business money is kept apart from yours. If they are mixed now, start with [how to separate business and personal money](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/separate-business-and-personal-money/).

How long to keep old records, and what tax records you need, depends on your situation. Ask an accountant. With clean records in hand, that conversation is much easier.
