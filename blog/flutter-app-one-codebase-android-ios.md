# Flutter Apps: One Codebase for Android and iPhone

What building with Flutter means for a business app, why one codebase saves money, and what drives the cost of an app up or down.

If you want an app for your business, the first surprise is usually that Android and iPhone are two different platforms. Traditionally that meant two apps, often built by two developers, and two bills. Flutter changes that. This guide explains what Flutter is, what one codebase means for you, and what actually decides what an app costs.

## The two-platform problem

Android phones and iPhones run different operating systems. An app written the traditional way for one will not run on the other. So a business that wanted both had to pay for the same app to be built twice.

The cost was not only at the start. Every change, bug fix and new feature had to be done twice too. Over time, the two versions drifted apart, with a button in one place on Android and somewhere else on iPhone.

## What Flutter is

Flutter is a toolkit made by Google for building apps. Developers write the app once, and Flutter builds it for both Android and iOS from the same source code. That shared source is what people mean by "one codebase".

Flutter apps are compiled into proper apps for each platform. They are installed from Google Play and the App Store like any other app, and to the person using them they behave like any other app on their phone.

## What one codebase means for your business

- **You pay for one build, not two.** The bulk of the work is done once.
- **Changes land on both phones at once.** Ask for a new screen or a fix, and it reaches Android and iPhone users together.
- **The app looks the same everywhere.** Your customers get the same layout and branding whichever phone they use.
- **One team understands the whole thing.** No coordinating between an Android developer and an iOS developer.

One codebase does not mean the app is free to run, and it does not make a complicated app simple. It removes the duplication, not the work.

## What makes an app cost more, or less

Apps vary far more than websites. An app that shows your menu and opening hours is a very different job from one where customers sign in, pay inside the app and get notifications.

**These push the cost up:**

- User accounts and logins
- Payments taken inside the app
- A server or database behind it
- Push notifications
- Working offline and syncing later
- Maps, chat or live tracking
- An admin panel for you to manage content

**These keep it down:**

- A small, clear set of screens
- Content you can already supply
- Existing branding to work from
- An existing website or API to connect to
- Launching on one store first
- Knowing what the app is for before work starts

If you can write down in plain words what the app does and who uses it, you have done the most useful preparation there is.

## Running costs to expect

Even with one codebase, a few costs sit outside the build:

- **Store accounts.** Google Play charges a one-off developer registration fee. Apple charges a yearly fee for its Developer Program.
- **Backend services.** If the app needs a server, a database or push notifications, those run on paid services.
- **Payment provider fees** if you take payments in the app.

Ask any developer to list these before you commit. A quote that only covers the build is only part of the picture.

## Do you need an app at all?

Be honest about this one. If customers mainly want to see your services, prices and contact details, a website does that job for far less, and nobody has to install anything. Apps make sense when people use them repeatedly: ordering often, tracking something, or logging in to an account.
