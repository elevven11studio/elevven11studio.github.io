How to Do a Stock Count in Your Shop

23 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Your records say you have 24 tins of milk. The shelf has 19. Where did five go? Sold without being rung up, damaged and thrown away, never delivered, or taken?

A stock count tells you the real number. Done regularly, it also tells you where the gap is coming from. Here is a simple method that works for small shops, with or without software.

Why counting matters

You cannot trust your profit figures if your stock figures are wrong. If records show more stock than you have, you think you are richer than you are. If they show less, you reorder things you do not need.

Counting also makes losses visible. A shop that never counts never knows what it is losing.

Full counts and spot counts

There are two kinds of count, and most shops need both.

  • Full count. Every item in the shop, once in a while. It takes time, so pick a quiet day or count after closing.
  • Spot count. One shelf, one category or a handful of fast-moving or expensive items, often. It takes minutes and catches problems early.

A good rhythm for a small shop is a weekly spot count of high-value or high-theft items, and a full count every month or quarter.

Before you count

  1. Pick a time when nothing is moving. Do not count while selling. If you must, count one section at a time and note the time.
  2. Record all deliveries first. Goods that arrived today but were not entered will look like extra stock.
  3. Record known losses. Broken, expired or damaged items should be written off with a note before the count, not discovered during it.
  4. Tidy the shelves. Put like items together. Bring stock from the store room to its section or count the store room separately.
  5. Print or write a count sheet. One line per item, with a blank for the counted quantity. Do not show the expected number on the sheet.

That last point matters. If the counter can see "24", they tend to find 24.

How to count

  • Count in pairs if you can. One counts, one writes. Swap for a second pass on expensive items.
  • Go shelf by shelf, left to right, top to bottom. Never jump around.
  • Count units, not cartons, unless every carton is sealed and full.
  • Mark each shelf as done so nothing is counted twice or skipped.
  • Weigh items sold by weight rather than guessing.

Compare and investigate

Once the count is done, compare counted quantity with what your records say. For each difference, ask:

DifferenceCommon causes
Shelf has lessSale not recorded, damage not written off, theft, delivery short
Shelf has moreDelivery not recorded, sale rung up as the wrong item, refund not put back
Two similar items, one over and one underWrong item scanned or picked at the till

Recount any big difference before you accept it. Then adjust the record to match the shelf, and write the reason.

Keep it right between counts

A count fixes the numbers for one day. Habits keep them right:

  • Every delivery is entered when it arrives, at its real cost.
  • Every loss is recorded with a reason.
  • Every sale goes through the till, even for friends and family.
  • Only certain people can change stock numbers.

How Counterbook helps

Counterbook is our point of sale app for shops. It is free for Windows, with Android coming soon, and it works offline with no account.

Stock only moves through records

In Counterbook, stock only changes through recorded movements: opening, receive, sale or refund, adjust or count, and loss. Each product has a stock history, so you can see what moved it and when.

Receiving goods

You raise a purchase order with a supplier, then receive it when the goods arrive. Stock goes up at the cost you enter, and the supplier's balance goes up if you have not paid. Profit uses the average cost from what you paid at each receipt, so entering real costs keeps your profit figures honest.

Counting and adjusting

When you count a shelf and find a difference, Counterbook records the adjustment. The Counterbook docs training includes a module on exactly this: count one shelf with a difference of two, read the adjustment, record a loss with a note, and check that every stock change has a reason.

If you keep counts in a spreadsheet, Counterbook can import stock counts from CSV or Excel. You match columns and read a dry run before you confirm.

Low stock warnings

Each product can have a low stock level. When stock falls to it, Counterbook warns you, so you reorder before you run out.

Who can touch stock

Roles keep stock changes in the right hands. The Warehouse Staff role handles products, receiving, adjusting and counting stock, and inventory reports. Stock losses also appear on the owner's watch list in the activity log, next to voids and price changes.

Start with one shelf

You do not need a perfect system on day one. This week, pick your ten most expensive items and count them. Compare with your records and find out why any differ. That one exercise usually shows you exactly where your shop is leaking.
