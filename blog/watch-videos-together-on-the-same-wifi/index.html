How to Watch Videos Together on the Same Wi-Fi

23 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

You have a movie on your laptop. Three friends want to watch it, but there is one small screen. Or you are in a hostel, a family house or an office, and everyone has their own phone. Passing a laptop around does not work, and counting "3, 2, 1, play" on four devices never stays in sync for long.

There is a better way: play the video from one device and let everyone else watch it on their own screen over the same Wi-Fi.

How local watch-together works

Your Wi-Fi router connects every device in the house, even when there is no internet. A watch-together app uses that local network:

  1. One device, the host, has the video file.
  2. The host streams it to every other device on the same network.
  3. Play, pause and seek on any screen are sent to all the others, so everyone stays on the same second.

Because the video moves across your own network, it does not use internet data. Nothing has to be uploaded, and guests do not need their own copy of the file.

What you need

  • One Wi-Fi network. A home router, an office router or a phone hotspot all work. Every device must join the same one.
  • A host device with the video. A laptop or phone with the file on it.
  • Guest devices. Phones, tablets, laptops or a smart TV.

Tips for smooth playback

Keep the host close to the router

The host is sending video to everyone, so it needs the best connection. Put it near the router, or connect a laptop by cable if you can.

Watch out for guest and isolated Wi-Fi

Some routers, and many hotel and office networks, keep devices apart for security. This is often called "guest mode" or "client isolation". If guests cannot find the host, this is usually why. Use a home network or a phone hotspot instead.

Choose files browsers can play

Most videos play everywhere, but some formats are harder. Many browsers cannot play HEVC, also called x265, video. If a guest watching in a browser keeps buffering, the file format is the likely cause.

Fewer guests on a weak router

An old router may struggle with five devices streaming at once. If playback stutters, try fewer devices or a lower quality file.

Only use networks you trust

On a shared network, other people may be able to see what is sent over it. Watch together on a network you control.

SyncPlay: our watch-together app

SyncPlay is a watch-together app we are building. It is in testing right now, for Windows, Android and Android TV. It is not available to download yet, but you can ask to be told when it launches on the SyncPlay page.

What SyncPlay does not do

SyncPlay does not work over the internet. Everyone must be on the same network as the host. If your friends are in another city, it is not the right tool.

Privacy

SyncPlay has no accounts, no cloud and no ads, and nothing is sent to Elevven11. App guests connect over an encrypted link checked against the host's own certificate. Browser guests join over the local network without encryption, which is one more reason to use a network you trust.

Poster lookups send film titles to free online sources, and you can turn that off in Settings. Rooms close on their own after a few hours.

If a browser guest keeps buffering because of an HEVC file, the host can see which guests are stuck and make a browser copy.

Before you start a watch party

  1. Get everyone on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot.
  2. Put the host device near the router and plug it in.
  3. Check the video plays on the host first.
  4. Load subtitles before people join, not halfway through.
  5. Send the join code or link, then press play once everyone is in.

Watching together on one network is simple once the host and router are set up properly. Keep an eye on the SyncPlay page for when testing ends.
