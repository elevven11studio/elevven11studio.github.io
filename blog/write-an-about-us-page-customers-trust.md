# How to Write an About Us Page Customers Trust

What to put on your About Us page, the tone that works for a small business, and three simple structures you can follow and fill in.

The About Us page is one of the most visited pages on a small business website, and one of the most neglected. Many say something like "We are a dynamic company committed to excellence." That tells a customer nothing.

People open your About page with a few real questions: Who is behind this? Are they real? Can they do what I need? Will they treat me well? A good page answers those in plain words.

## What to include

You do not need all of these, but most good About pages have most of them.

- **Who you are.** Your name, or the names of the people the customer will deal with. A business with a face feels safer than a logo alone.
- **What you do and for whom.** One clear sentence. "We make school uniforms for private schools in Abuja" is better than "We provide quality fashion solutions."
- **How long you have been doing it.** Only if it is true. Even "since 2023" is fine.
- **Where you are.** Your area or full address if customers visit, and the areas you deliver to or serve.
- **Why you are different.** One or two real reasons, such as same-day turnaround or a specific skill. Not "excellent customer service", which everyone claims.
- **Proof.** Photos of your shop, team or work, a few customer reviews, or well-known clients if they agree to be named.
- **What to do next.** A clear way to reach you: a WhatsApp button, phone number or contact form.

## What to leave out

- Mission and vision statements that sound like a bank's annual report.
- Words that mean nothing on their own: "innovative", "world-class", "best in town".
- Long histories. Two or three sentences about how you started is plenty.
- Anything you cannot back up. If you say "fast delivery", say how fast.

## Getting the tone right

Write the way you would speak to a customer standing at your counter. Use "we" and "you". Keep sentences short.

Compare these two openings:

> Our organisation is dedicated to providing premium bakery products to discerning clients across the FCT.

> We are a small family bakery in Kubwa. We bake fresh every morning, and we make custom cakes for birthdays, weddings and office events.

The second one is shorter, more specific and sounds like real people. That is what builds trust.

If English is not how you usually talk to customers, it is fine to keep it simple. Clear beats clever.

## Three structures you can follow

Pick the one that fits your business and fill in the brackets with your own details.

### The short version

Good for a one-page site where About is a small section.

1. One sentence on what you do and for whom.
2. One sentence on where you are and how long you have been doing it.
3. One sentence on what makes you different.
4. A button to contact you.

For example: "[Business name] sells and repairs phones and laptops in [area]. We have been fixing devices since [year]. Most repairs are done the same day while you wait. [Chat on WhatsApp]"

### The founder story

Good for personal businesses like salons, tailoring, catering or consulting, where customers choose the person as much as the service.

1. A photo of you at work.
2. Who you are and how you got started, in three or four sentences.
3. What you do now and who you do it for.
4. What a customer can expect when they work with you.
5. A few reviews.
6. How to book or order.

Keep the story about why it matters to the customer. "I trained for two years before opening" tells them you are skilled. A long personal history does not.

### The team and process version

Good for businesses with staff or a clear way of working, like clinics, schools, agencies or building contractors.

1. One paragraph on what the business does.
2. The key people, each with a photo, name, role and one line on their experience.
3. How you work, as three to five simple steps from first contact to finished job.
4. Proof: certifications you actually hold, clients, or reviews.
5. Contact details and opening hours.

The steps reassure people who are nervous about the process. They know what happens after they get in touch.

## Use real photos

Stock photos of smiling people in suits are easy to spot and they make a small business look fake. A clear phone photo of your real shop, team or work does far more. For tips, read [how to take better product photos with your phone](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/take-better-product-photos-with-your-phone/).

## Keep it current

Update the page when something changes: a new location, new staff, new services, a new phone number. An old address on your About page sends customers to the wrong place, and that is worse than having no page.

## Where the About page fits

On a one-page site, About is usually a short section near the top or middle. On a larger site it gets its own page.
