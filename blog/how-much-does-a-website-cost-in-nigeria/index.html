How Much Does a Small Business Website Cost in Nigeria?

23 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

Ask five web designers what a website costs and you will get five very different answers, and most leave out the fees you will pay every month after launch. This guide breaks down where the money actually goes so you can compare quotes properly.

The three costs every website has

Every website, no matter who builds it, has the same three parts:

  1. The build. Design, writing the pages and making them work on phones. This is usually a one-time fee.
  2. Hosting. The server that keeps your site online. Many builders charge this monthly.
  3. The domain. Your address, like yourbusiness.com.ng. This is a yearly fee paid to a registrar.

When two quotes look very different, the difference is usually in the second and third parts, not the first.

Why there is no monthly hosting fee

Most small business sites are static: pages of text, images and contact links that do not change every minute. A static site can be hosted on GitHub Pages, which is free for eligible sites. We set it up for you as part of every package.

That is a real saving. Paid hosting is billed every month or year for as long as the site is online, so over a few years it can cost more than the site itself. If you want the details, read how free hosting works.

Costs to plan for

A fair quote tells you about these up front:

  • A custom domain. Connecting a .com or .com.ng domain is an NGN 15,000 add-on. The domain registration itself is billed at cost, and you renew it yearly.
  • Changes after launch. Each package includes a free update period, from two weeks on Starter to three months on Custom. Small text and image changes count as free updates. New pages or new features are quoted separately. See what counts as a website update.
  • Features that need a database. Logins, bookings and online payments need more than a static site, so they are quoted per project.

How to compare quotes

When you get a quote from anyone, ask these four questions:

  1. Is this a one-time fee, or is there a monthly charge?
  2. Who owns the domain, me or you?
  3. How long are changes free, and what does a change cost after that?
  4. Does the site work well on a phone?

If a quote cannot answer all four clearly, the low price might not stay low.

The short answer

For most small businesses in Nigeria, a good website costs between NGN 50,000 and NGN 120,000 one time, plus a domain if you want your own address. You should not need to pay a monthly fee for a simple site.
