How to Spot Fake Product Reviews When Shopping Online

23 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Reviews are the closest thing online shopping has to asking a friend. That is exactly why some sellers fake them, pay for them, or quietly bury the bad ones. A product with hundreds of glowing reviews can still arrive broken.

There is no single test that proves a review is fake. What you can do is look for patterns, weigh the evidence, and lean on the reviews that are hardest to fake.

Read the words, not the stars

Real buyers write about their own experience. Fake or paid reviews tend to sound like adverts.

Be more doubtful of reviews that:

  • Praise without detail. "Great product, highly recommend, five stars" could describe anything.
  • Repeat the product name in full, as if written for search engines.
  • Use the same phrases as other reviews on the page, or the same odd spelling mistakes.
  • Describe the wrong thing. A review praising the battery life of a phone case was probably moved from another listing.
  • Talk about delivery and packaging only. That tells you about the courier, not the product.

Trust reviews that mention specifics: what size they ordered and how it fitted, how long they have used it, what went wrong and how the seller handled it. Mixed reviews that praise one thing and criticise another are among the most believable.

Look at when reviews arrived

Many marketplaces show the date of each review. Sort by newest, or scroll through, and notice the rhythm.

A product that sells steadily usually gets reviews steadily. A sudden cluster of five star reviews within a few days, especially soon after the listing went up or right after a run of complaints, is worth noting.

Keep this in proportion. Real buyers also arrive in bursts after a sale or a promotion, and they write short reviews too. A burst is a reason to read more carefully, not a verdict.

Check the star breakdown

Most stores show how many reviews gave each star rating. Look at the shape rather than the average.

What you seeWhat it may suggest
Mostly 5 stars, some 4, a few 1 to 3A typical picture for a product people like
Almost all 5 stars and a large share of 1 starOpinions are split. Read the 1 star reviews for a repeated fault
Many recent 1 star reviews under an old high averageQuality may have changed, or a new batch is worse

Also weigh the average by how many people stand behind it. A high score from a handful of buyers can move with one or two reviews. Our guide on how to check a product listing before you buy covers that in more detail.

Treat photo reviews with care

Photos from buyers are valuable because they show the real item in a real home. They are also harder to fake well. Look for:

  • Ordinary settings. A product on a kitchen table or in a hand is more believable than a perfect white background.
  • Consistency. Does the colour, size and finish match the listing photos? If buyers' photos all look different from the seller's, the seller's are probably flattering.
  • Repeats. The same photo appearing in several reviews, or one that looks like the listing's own pictures, is a warning.

If a photo looks too polished, you can search it with Google Lens or another reverse image search to see if it appears elsewhere online.

Look for signs of paid reviews

Some reviews admit they were rewarded, with lines like "I received this product for free in exchange for my honest review". That does not make the review false, but people who get things free tend to be kinder. Give them less weight.

Where a store marks reviews from verified purchases, give those more weight than unverified ones. And if a seller's packaging or a message offers you a refund or gift in exchange for a five star review, assume others accepted it.

Using ShopInspect on the reviews

ShopInspect is a free Chrome extension we built to give you a second opinion on the listing in front of you. Open a product page and press Ctrl+Shift+Y, click the toolbar button, or right-click the page.

The Reviews check weighs the rating by how many buyers stand behind it, shows the star breakdown, and summarises what the visible reviews praise and complain about. That saves you scrolling through pages of reviews to find the repeated complaint.

It also looks for review patterns, but it is careful about them. None is ever raised above minor, because real buyers write short reviews in bursts too, and ShopInspect says it cannot tell the difference. It reads the reviews in your browser and keeps only themes and a few short quotes for the report. No reviewer's name, avatar or profile is read.

The practical rule

Do not trust the average. Read the specific reviews, check the dates and the star breakdown, give photo reviews from ordinary homes extra weight, and look for the complaint that keeps coming back. If the reviews make you doubt the seller as well as the product, read how to tell if an online seller is legit.
