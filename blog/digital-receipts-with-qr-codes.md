# Digital Receipts With QR Codes: Do Small Shops Need Them?

Why receipts matter for small shops, how paper and digital receipts compare, and how a QR code on a receipt gives customers a copy on their phone.

Plenty of small shops do not give receipts unless the customer asks. It feels like extra work for a small sale. But receipts protect the shop as much as the customer, and the choice is no longer just paper or nothing.

## Why receipts matter

A receipt is a record that a sale happened, for how much and how it was paid. That helps in more ways than it looks.

- **Returns and complaints.** A customer brings back an item. Did they buy it here, when, at what price? A receipt settles it in seconds.
- **Trust.** Customers who get a clear receipt feel the price was fair and the shop is serious.
- **Business buyers.** Companies, schools and churches often need a receipt for every purchase they make.
- **Cash control.** When every sale produces a receipt, a sale that never got rung up is easier to notice.
- **Your own records.** Receipts match what was sold with what was paid, which makes counts and reports easier to check.

If you sell to businesses on account, you may also need invoices. See [how to write an invoice](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/how-to-write-an-invoice/) for the difference.

## Paper receipts

Most shops use small thermal printers with 58 mm or 80 mm paper rolls.

**Good:** fast, familiar, and the customer leaves with something in hand.

**Not so good:** paper rolls cost money, thermal print fades over time, and most receipts end up in the bin or lost in a bag. When the customer comes back weeks later, the receipt is often gone.

## Digital receipts

A digital receipt is a copy the customer keeps on their phone. It can be sent as an image, a PDF or a link, or opened by scanning a code.

**Good:** it does not fade, it is easy to find later, and it can be forwarded to whoever pays the bills.

**Not so good:** sending it by WhatsApp or email usually means asking for the customer's number or address, which some people would rather not give.

## Where a QR code fits

A QR code printed on a paper receipt gives you both. The customer leaves with paper, and if they want a lasting copy, they scan the code with their phone camera. No need to share a phone number.

When you choose this kind of system, check a few things:

- Does the customer need to install an app to see the receipt?
- What personal details show on the digital copy?
- Is the receipt stored on someone's server, and who can see it?
- Does it work if your shop has no internet?

## How receipts work in Counterbook

[Counterbook](https://elevven11studio.github.io/counterbook/) is our point of sale app for small shops. It is free for Windows, with Android coming soon, and it works with no internet and no account.

### Print or share

Receipts print on 58 mm or 80 mm paper. You set the paper width, your phone, address and a footer line when you set up the business. On the receipt screen, tap Share to send an image, PDF, link or text, or save an image or PDF to the device.

### The QR code on every receipt

Every receipt carries a QR code. Here is what happens when it is scanned:

| Who scans | What happens |
|---|---|
| The customer | A digital copy opens in their phone's browser, with no app. They can keep it or save it as a PDF. |
| You, at the till | The sale opens, ready to refund or reprint. This works only for sales recorded in your own shop. |

The digital copy shows the shop, receipt number, date, items, totals and payment, but no customer or staff name.

### Nothing stored online

The receipt travels inside the link itself and is not stored online. Your shop does not need to be online for it to work. The customer's phone needs a recent Chrome, Safari, Firefox or Edge and an internet connection to open the page.

A very long sale shows totals only on the digital copy, and a very small code carries just a reference.

### Reprints are controlled

Reprinting a receipt needs permission and is logged. Receipt reprints appear on the owner's watch list in the activity log, because an old receipt can be used to fake a refund. The [Counterbook docs](https://elevven11studio.github.io/counterbook/docs/) explain the permissions for each role.

## Tips for any shop

Whatever system you use:

1. **Give a receipt for every sale,** not only when asked.
2. **Keep the shop's name and contact on it,** so customers can reach you.
3. **Leave out what is not needed.** A receipt does not need the customer's name to do its job.
4. **Use a clear returns line** in the footer, such as how many days customers have to return goods.
5. **Watch reprints and refunds.** Both are common ways cash disappears.
