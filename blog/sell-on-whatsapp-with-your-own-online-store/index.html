How to Sell on WhatsApp With Your Own Online Store

23 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Many Nigerian businesses already sell on WhatsApp, and it works. Customers trust it, replies are fast, and there is no app to install. The problem is not WhatsApp. The problem is doing the whole sale inside a chat. This guide looks at what goes wrong with DM-only selling and how a small store site fixes it while keeping WhatsApp at the centre.

What selling only through DMs really costs

If you sell through Instagram or WhatsApp messages alone, you will recognise this:

  • The same questions, all day. "How much?" "Is it available?" "Do you have it in black?" Every answer takes your time.
  • Lost orders. A customer asks a question at night, you reply in the morning, and they have bought elsewhere.
  • Wrong totals. Adding up four items plus delivery in your head, while replying to three other people, leads to mistakes.
  • Messy delivery fees. You quote one fee to one customer and a different fee to another for the same area.
  • Nowhere to send new customers. A screenshot of a catalogue is not something people can browse or share easily.

None of these mean you should stop using WhatsApp. They mean the browsing, choosing and adding up should happen somewhere else.

How a WhatsApp checkout store works

A WhatsApp checkout store is a normal website with your products on it. The difference is what happens when the customer is ready to buy:

  1. The customer browses your products, picks sizes or colours, and adds items to a cart.
  2. The site works out the total, including the delivery fee for their area.
  3. They tap checkout, and WhatsApp opens with the full order already typed: items, quantities, total and delivery details.
  4. They send it to you, and you confirm payment and delivery in the chat as you already do.

You still close the sale in WhatsApp. You just start the conversation with a complete, correct order instead of a question.

What to prepare before you build a store

Whoever builds your store, get these ready first. It will speed things up and force some useful decisions:

  • Product list. Name, price and a short description for each item.
  • Photos. Clear, well-lit pictures on a plain background. Consistent photos make a small shop look established.
  • Options. Which products come in sizes or colours, and does the price change between them?
  • Categories. How customers would naturally group your items, like bags, shoes and accessories.
  • Delivery zones. The areas you deliver to and the fee for each. Write them down once and stop negotiating them per customer.
  • Pickup and minimum order. Whether customers can collect, and whether there is a minimum spend for delivery.

If you can fill in this list, you are ready for a store.

A marketplace brings traffic but puts your products beside cheaper alternatives. DMs keep things personal but do not scale. Your own store gives you a link to put in your bio, on flyers and on your WhatsApp status that sends people straight to a full catalogue.

Getting customers to your store

A store only helps if people find it. Put the link in your Instagram and TikTok bios, your WhatsApp Business profile and every status post. A QR code on packaging and flyers works well for repeat customers, and we offer a print-ready one as an NGN 5,000 add-on.
