How to Calculate VAT in Nigeria: Adding and Removing It

23 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

VAT is a tax added to the price of goods and services. If your business charges it, you need to get two calculations right: adding VAT to a price, and working out how much VAT is inside a total that already includes it.

The first is easy. The second is where most people make a mistake.

The rate

At the time of writing, the standard VAT rate in Nigeria is 7.5%. Some goods and services are exempt or zero-rated, and businesses above the registration threshold must charge and report VAT. Rules change, so confirm the current rate and what applies to your business with the tax authority or an accountant before you rely on any figure here.

The examples below use 7.5%. The method works for any rate.

Adding VAT to a price

Start with the price before VAT, sometimes called the net price.

  • VAT = price before VAT x rate / 100
  • Price with VAT = price before VAT + VAT

A quicker way to get the total in one step is to multiply by 1 plus the rate as a decimal. At 7.5% that is 1.075.

Example: adding VAT

You charge NGN 20,000 for a service before VAT.

  1. VAT is 20,000 x 7.5 / 100, which is NGN 1,500.
  2. Price with VAT is 20,000 + 1,500, which is NGN 21,500.

Check: 20,000 x 1.075 is also 21,500.

Removing VAT from a total

Now the other direction. You have a receipt or a quote for NGN 53,750 that already includes VAT, and you need to know the price before VAT and how much of it is tax.

  • Price before VAT = price with VAT / (1 + rate / 100)
  • VAT = price with VAT minus price before VAT

Example: removing VAT

  1. Price before VAT is 53,750 / 1.075, which is NGN 50,000.
  2. VAT is 53,750 minus 50,000, which is NGN 3,750.

Check it forwards: 50,000 x 7.5 / 100 is 3,750, and 50,000 + 3,750 is 53,750.

The common mistake

The tempting shortcut is to take 7.5% of the total and subtract it. That gives the wrong answer.

On the NGN 53,750 total, 7.5% is NGN 4,031.25. Subtract it and you get NGN 49,718.75, which is NGN 281.25 less than the real price before VAT.

The reason is that VAT was calculated on the price before VAT, not on the total. The total is already 107.5% of the original price, so you have to divide by 1.075 to get back to 100%. Subtracting a percentage of the bigger number always takes off too much.

The error looks small on one sale, but across a month of receipts it throws off both your income figures and the VAT you report.

Where VAT fits on an invoice

If you charge VAT, show it on its own line rather than hiding it in the price. A clear invoice lists:

LineAmount
SubtotalNGN 50,000
VAT (7.5%)NGN 3,750
Total dueNGN 53,750

If you give a discount, take it off the subtotal first, then charge VAT on what remains. Our guide on how to write an invoice covers the other details an invoice needs.

Keep the VAT separate

The VAT you collect from customers is not your income. It is money you hold for the tax authority. Many small businesses keep it in a separate account so it is ready when it is due, rather than spending it as part of the day's takings.

When you work out profit, use the price before VAT. Using the VAT-inclusive total makes your sales and margins look bigger than they are. See profit margin vs markup for how to check your pricing.
