# How to Tell If an Online Seller Is Legit Before You Pay

Check how long a seller has been around, their contact details, return policy, how they want to be paid and whether the price is believable.

Buying from a big marketplace or a store you know is one thing. Buying from a new website, an Instagram page or a seller in a WhatsApp group is another. Many small sellers are honest and good. Some take the money and disappear.

You cannot be certain about any seller, but a few checks sort most of them quickly. Do them before you pay, not after.

## How long have they been around?

Scam stores tend to be new, because they get reported and shut down. An honest business usually leaves a trail.

- **On a marketplace,** look at the seller's profile for how long they have been selling and how many ratings they have.
- **On a website,** search the domain name in a free WHOIS lookup to see when it was registered. A store registered last month offering huge discounts deserves caution.
- **On social media,** scroll back through the posts. A page with years of posts, real customer comments and tagged photos is more reassuring than one that appeared recently with many followers and no conversation.
- **Search the name** with words like "scam" or "review" and see what other buyers say.

## Can you reach a real person?

A legitimate seller wants you to be able to contact them. Look for:

- A phone number that someone answers.
- A physical address you can check on a map, ideally with a Google Business Profile and reviews.
- An email address on the business's own domain, or at least a consistent business name.

Be cautious when the only contact is a WhatsApp number or a form, when the address is vague, or when the seller will not answer simple questions about the product. Call before you pay. How someone responds to "Can you send me a video of the item?" tells you a lot.

## Is there a return policy, and is it real?

Find the return or refund policy before buying. A good one says how many days you have, who pays to send the item back, and how refunds are paid.

Warning signs:

- No policy anywhere, or "no returns" on everything.
- A policy copied from another store, sometimes still mentioning the other store's name or country.
- Vague promises like "100% satisfaction guaranteed" with no process behind them.

If you cannot find a policy, ask in writing. A screenshot of the seller's answer is useful later if something goes wrong.

## How do they want to be paid?

The payment method is often the clearest signal of all.

| Safer | Riskier |
|---|---|
| Pay on delivery, after checking the item | Full transfer before you see anything |
| Card payment through a known checkout | Transfer to a personal account whose name does not match the business |
| Marketplace checkout with buyer protection | Paying outside the marketplace "to save fees" |
| A receipt or invoice for what you paid | Gift cards, crypto or airtime as payment |

A seller who insists on one risky method and pushes you to pay quickly is a seller to walk away from. Card payments and marketplace checkouts usually give you some way to dispute a charge, so ask your bank or the platform how that works before you rely on it.

## Is the price believable?

A brand new phone at half the price every other shop charges is not a bargain. It is either fake, stolen, faulty, or not coming. Compare prices at two or three known stores. Small differences are normal. Huge ones need an explanation.

Also check the product photos. Search them with a reverse image search. If they come straight from the manufacturer or another shop, the seller may not have the item at all. Asking for a fresh photo with today's date written on paper beside it is a fair request.

## Make sure the shop is the real one

Some fake stores copy a well known shop's design and name. If you reached the store from an advert or a message, read the address carefully. ## Checking the listing with ShopInspect

Once you are happy with the store, [ShopInspect](https://elevven11studio.github.io/shopinspect/) helps with the listing itself. It is a free Chrome extension we built. Open a product page and press `Ctrl+Shift+Y`.

The **Seller** check shows who is selling the item, their rating and location where shown, and whether it is a marketplace seller or the store itself. The **Delivery and returns** check shows the delivery charge, an estimated total when both figures were read, the delivery estimate, the return window and the warranty. It also checks whether an advertised discount matches the prices shown.

ShopInspect looks at the listing, not the site, so it does not tell you whether a store is a scam. For phishing and fake stores, its own page points to [WebGuard](https://elevven11studio.github.io/webguard/). If a listing does not mention a return window, ShopInspect reports it as not stated rather than absent, because many stores keep policies on another page.

## Before you send money

Ask yourself: Have they been around a while? Can I reach them? Is there a return policy? Is the payment method one I can dispute? Is the price believable? If two or more answers are no, keep shopping. If you sell online yourself, these are also the things your customers check.
