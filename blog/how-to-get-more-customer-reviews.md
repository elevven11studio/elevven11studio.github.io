# How to Get More Customer Reviews for Your Business

When to ask customers for reviews, how to make it easy with links and QR codes, and how to reply to bad reviews without making things worse.

Before most people visit a new shop, salon or restaurant, they look it up. If they find a business with plenty of recent, good reviews and another with none, the choice is easy.

Most happy customers never leave a review. Not because they do not want to, but because nobody asked and it was not easy. Fix those two things and reviews start to come in.

## Where reviews matter most

For local businesses, the most useful place to collect reviews is your Google Business Profile. Those are the reviews people see when they search for you or look at Google Maps.

If you have not set one up yet, start with our [Google Business Profile setup guide](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/google-business-profile-setup-guide/). The rest of this post assumes you have a profile and can get its review link.

Reviews on Facebook or on your own website help too, but pick one main place and focus on it. Spreading customers across five sites leaves each one looking empty.

## Ask at the right moment

Timing is most of it. Ask when the customer is happiest:

- Right after they thank you or say something nice.
- When a job is finished and they have seen the result, like a haircut, a repaired phone or a delivered cake.
- When a regular comes back for the third or fourth time.

Do not wait a month. By then the good feeling has faded and your message gets ignored.

## Ask in a simple, personal way

A short, direct request works better than a long one. For example:

> Thank you for coming in today. If you were happy with the service, would you leave us a quick review on Google? It really helps a small business like ours. Here is the link.

Say it in person and send the link straight after, so it is waiting on their phone. People who have just heard you ask are much more likely to follow through.

A few things to avoid:

- **Do not offer money, discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews.** Google's review policies do not allow it, and reviews gathered that way can be removed.
- **Do not ask only the customers you think will be positive.** Ask everyone you served properly.
- **Do not write reviews yourself** or ask friends who never bought from you. Customers can often tell, and it breaks the trust you are trying to build.

## Make it take ten seconds

Every extra step loses people. Your job is to put the review form one tap away.

- **Use the direct review link.** Your Google Business Profile lets you copy a link that opens the review form for your business. Save it as a WhatsApp quick reply so you can send it in two taps.
- **Print a QR code.** Turn the link into a QR code with our free [QR code generator](https://elevven11studio.github.io/tools/qr-code-generator/), and put it on the counter, the table, the waiting area or your receipts. Add a short line like "Enjoyed your visit? Scan to review us."
- **Add it to your website** next to your contact details.

For ideas on where to place QR codes so people actually scan them, read [QR codes for small business](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/qr-codes-for-small-business/).

## Reply to every review

Replies are read by future customers, not just the reviewer. They show that a real person runs the business and cares.

For good reviews, keep it short and specific:

> Thank you, Ada. Glad the dress was ready in time for the wedding. See you next time.

That is enough. Avoid pasting the same "Thanks for your review!" under every one.

## How to handle a bad review

A bad review feels personal. Wait until you are calm before you reply. Then:

1. **Thank them and acknowledge the problem.** "Sorry your order arrived late" is better than defending yourself.
2. **Keep it short.** Two or three sentences. Long explanations look like excuses.
3. **Do not argue or share private details** like their order history or phone number.
4. **Offer to fix it privately.** Give a phone number or WhatsApp line and ask them to reach out.
5. **Fix the actual problem** if the complaint was fair.

An example:

> We are sorry the delivery was two hours late, that is not the service we aim for. Please message us on WhatsApp so we can make it right.

If a review is fake or from someone who was never your customer, you can report it through your Business Profile. Still reply calmly, because other people see it while it is there.

A few honest complaints among many good reviews do not scare customers away. A calm, helpful reply can actually win trust.

## Make it a habit

Reviews come from routine, not one big push. Pick one simple habit and keep it:

| Business | When to ask |
|---|---|
| Salon or barber | After the customer checks the mirror and is happy |
| Restaurant | When the bill comes, with a QR code on the table |
| Repair shop | When the customer picks up the working item |
| Online seller | A day after delivery, by WhatsApp |

A steady trickle of new reviews looks better than a burst from two years ago.
