# How to Calculate a Discount: Sale Price and Percent Off

The formulas for a sale price, the percent off, stacked discounts and finding the original price, with worked examples and what a discount does to your margin.

A discount looks simple until you have to put a number on the shelf. How much is 20% off? What discount did that shop really give you? And if you run two promotions at once, is 20% plus 10% the same as 30%?

It is not. Here is how to get each one right.

## The basic formula

A discount takes a percentage off the original price. What is left is the sale price.

- **Amount saved** = original price x discount / 100
- **Sale price** = original price minus amount saved

You can also do it in one step. Multiply the original price by (1 minus the discount as a decimal). For 20% off, that is 0.80.

### Example: 20% off a shirt

A shirt is NGN 15,000 and you offer 20% off.

1. Amount saved is 15,000 x 20 / 100, which is **NGN 3,000**.
2. Sale price is 15,000 minus 3,000, which is **NGN 12,000**.

Check: 15,000 x 0.80 is also 12,000.

## Working out the percent off

Sometimes you have both prices and want to know the discount. A supplier drops a carton from NGN 8,000 to NGN 6,000. How good is that?

- **Discount** = (original minus sale) / original x 100

1. The difference is 8,000 minus 6,000, which is NGN 2,000.
2. 2,000 divided by 8,000 is 0.25, so the discount is **25%**.

Always divide by the original price, not the sale price. Dividing 2,000 by 6,000 gives 33.3%, which makes the deal look bigger than it is.

## Stacked discounts do not add up

Say you take 20% off for a sale, then give loyal customers another 10% at the till. Many people assume that is 30% off. It is less, because the second discount is taken from the price that is already reduced.

Back to the NGN 15,000 shirt:

1. 20% off brings it to NGN 12,000.
2. 10% off 12,000 is 1,200, so the price becomes **NGN 10,800**.
3. The total saving is 15,000 minus 10,800, which is NGN 4,200.
4. 4,200 divided by 15,000 is **28%**, not 30%.

A quick way to combine two discounts: multiply the decimals left after each one. 0.80 x 0.90 is 0.72, so the customer pays 72% and saves 28%.

The order does not matter. 10% off first and then 20% off still ends at NGN 10,800.

## Finding the original price from a sale price

You see a tag that says NGN 9,000 after 25% off and want to know the old price. The tempting move is to add 25% back on: 9,000 x 1.25 is NGN 11,250. That is wrong, because the 25% was taken from the original, not from 9,000.

Divide instead:

- **Original price** = sale price / (1 minus discount as a decimal)

So 9,000 divided by 0.75 is **NGN 12,000**. Check it: 25% of 12,000 is 3,000, and 12,000 minus 3,000 is 9,000.

This is also how you spot a was-price that does not match the discount on the label.

## What a discount does to your margin

This is the part that catches shop owners out. A discount comes straight off your profit, not off your cost.

Say an item costs you NGN 7,000 and you sell it for NGN 10,000. Your profit is NGN 3,000 and your margin is 30%.

Now take 20% off:

1. The sale price is NGN 8,000.
2. Your cost is still NGN 7,000, so profit is **NGN 1,000**.
3. Your margin falls to 1,000 divided by 8,000, which is **12.5%**.

A 20% discount cut your profit per item by two thirds. To earn the same NGN 30,000 you made from selling 10 at full price, you now need to sell 30.

| Discount | Sale price | Profit per item | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | NGN 10,000 | NGN 3,000 | 30% |
| 10% | NGN 9,000 | NGN 2,000 | 22.2% |
| 20% | NGN 8,000 | NGN 1,000 | 12.5% |
| 30% | NGN 7,000 | NGN 0 | 0% |

At 30% off you are selling at cost. Anything deeper is a loss on every sale. On any item, the largest discount you can give without losing money is your margin before the discount.

Discounts can still be worth it. Clearing old stock, winning a regular customer or moving a slow line may matter more than one sale's profit. Just know the cost before you print the sign. ## Discounts and VAT

If you charge VAT, apply the discount first, then add tax to the reduced price. On a NGN 10,000 item with 10% off and 7.5% VAT, the sale price is NGN 9,000 and VAT is NGN 675, for a total of NGN 9,675. The [VAT guide](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/how-to-calculate-vat-in-nigeria/) covers the tax side in detail.
