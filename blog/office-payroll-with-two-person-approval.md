# Why Office Payroll Needs Two People, Not One

Why small offices should have one person prepare payroll and another approve it, what records to keep and how payslips protect you and your staff.

In many small offices, one person does payroll from start to finish. They work out each salary, prepare the bank list and send it for payment. It is quick, and it works until a mistake slips through, or until someone adds a name that should not be there.

The fix is simple and used by every serious organization: one person prepares payroll, and a different person approves it.

## What can go wrong with one person

When a single person controls payroll, nobody checks their work. Common problems include:

- **Honest errors.** A wrong figure, an allowance paid twice, a loan deduction forgotten.
- **Ghost workers.** A name that stays on the list after someone leaves, or a name that was never staff.
- **Quiet raises.** A salary changed without anyone else knowing.
- **No defence.** If a staff member complains about pay, the preparer has nobody to confirm the run was checked.

Two-person approval protects the preparer as much as the business. It is not about distrust. It is about having a second pair of eyes on money.

## How two-person approval works

1. **The preparer** updates staff pay details for the month: new staff, leavers, allowances, loans and deductions. They prepare the payroll.
2. **The approver** reviews it. They compare it with last month, check new and removed names and ask about anything unusual.
3. If something is wrong, the approver sends it back. The preparer fixes it.
4. Once approved, the run is locked. Payment goes out from the approved list only.

The approver should not be able to edit the payroll. If they can change figures and approve them, you are back to one person.

## What the approver should check

A good approval takes 15 to 30 minutes for a small office. Look at:

- **Headcount.** Does the number of staff match last month, plus joiners, minus leavers?
- **Totals.** Is the total pay close to last month? If not, why?
- **Changes.** Any salary that moved should have a written reason.
- **Bank details.** A changed account number is a classic fraud sign. Confirm it with the staff member directly.
- **Missing IDs.** Staff without a tax ID or pension details cause trouble when you remit deductions.

## Give every staff member a payslip

A payslip shows each person how their pay was worked out: basic pay, allowances, deductions and net pay. It helps staff:

- Check their own pay and raise errors early.
- Prove income for loans, rent or visas.
- Understand what was deducted and why.

For you, payslips cut down on "why is my salary short?" conversations, because the answer is on paper.

## Keep the records

Keep each month's approved payroll, the payslips and the bank schedule together. Do not change a payroll after it has been approved and paid. If there was a mistake, correct it in the next month's run and note why. That way the history always shows what was actually paid.

Store these records where only HR, accounts and management can see them. Pay is private.

## Tax and deductions

Payroll deductions in Nigeria include PAYE tax and pension, and sometimes NHF. The rates and bands change with the law, so always have an accountant confirm the figures you use. Do not copy rates from a blog post, including this one.

## Start with your next payroll

You do not need software to begin. For next month:

1. Choose a preparer and a different approver.
2. Give the approver a checklist: headcount, totals, changes, bank details.
3. Keep the approved list, payslips and bank schedule in one file.
4. Never edit a run after payment. Fix it next month.
