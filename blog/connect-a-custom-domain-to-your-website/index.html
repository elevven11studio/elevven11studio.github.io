How to Get and Connect a Custom Domain to Your Website

23 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Your domain is your address on the internet, like yourbusiness.com.ng. It goes on your flyers, your WhatsApp profile, your invoices and your shop sign. Getting it right once saves you from reprinting everything later. This guide covers how to pick one, why you should own it, and what connecting it to your site involves.

Do you need a custom domain at all?

Not on day one. Every site hosted on GitHub Pages gets a free default address, and it works. Plenty of businesses launch on it.

A custom domain helps in three ways:

  • It looks established. kemisbags.com.ng reads as a real business. A long default address reads as a side project.
  • It is easy to say and remember. Customers can type it from a flyer or hear it over the phone.
  • It moves with you. If you change web designers or hosting later, your address stays the same.

If you print anything with your web address on it, get the domain first.

.com or .com.ng?

Both work, and both are recognised by customers. The choice depends on who you serve.

.com.com.ng
SignalsInternational, generalNigerian business
Good forBusinesses with customers abroad or online-only brandsLocal shops, services and businesses serving Nigerians

If your customers are mostly in Nigeria, .com.ng tells them straight away that you are local. If you sell abroad or plan to, .com is the safer choice. If both are available and affordable, some businesses register both and point one at the other, so nobody lands on someone else's site by mistake.

How to pick a good name

  1. Keep it short. Fewer characters means fewer typos.
  2. Avoid hyphens and numbers. They are hard to say out loud. "Kemi hyphen bags" will be misheard.
  3. Match your business name. Customers should be able to guess it.
  4. Say it to someone. If they cannot spell it after hearing it once, try another.
  5. Check social handles. Matching your Instagram name to your domain makes you easier to find.

Why you must own your domain

This is the most important part of the guide. The domain should be registered in your name or your business's name, not your web designer's.

Some businesses find out too late that their designer registered the domain under their own account. When they part ways, the business either loses its address or has to negotiate to get it back.

When anyone sets up a domain for you, ask:

  • Whose name is the registration in?
  • Can I log in to the registrar account myself?
  • When does it renew, and who gets the reminder?

You should be able to answer all three.

Registration and renewal

Domains are rented, not bought outright. You register a name with a domain registrar for a period, usually a year, then renew it. Prices vary between registrars and between extensions, so compare a few before you pay.

Watch the renewal price as well as the first-year price. Some registrars offer a cheap first year and charge more on renewal.

If a domain is not renewed, it expires. After a grace period it can be registered by someone else, and your site, links and printed materials stop working. Turn on auto-renewal if your registrar offers it, and make sure the reminder emails go to an address you check.

What "connecting" a domain means

Registering a domain gives you the name. Connecting it tells the internet where your website lives. This is done by adding DNS records at your registrar that point the domain to your host, then telling the host to expect that domain.

It is not difficult, but the details matter. A wrong record can leave your site unreachable or showing a security warning. Changes can also take some time to spread across the internet, so a site may not appear on the new address immediately.

Quick checklist

  • Decide between .com and .com.ng based on where your customers are
  • Pick a short name with no hyphens
  • Register it in your own name
  • Note the renewal date and turn on auto-renewal
  • Connect it before you print anything with your web address on it
