# What to Prepare Before Getting a Website

A simple checklist of the logo, photos, text, contact details and domain decisions to sort out before a designer starts building your website.

The slowest part of building a small business website is rarely the building. It is waiting for content. The designer asks for photos, the owner says "next week", and a job that could take days takes a month.

If you gather a few things before you start, your site goes live faster and looks closer to what you pictured. Here is what to prepare.

## 1. Your basic business details

Write these down in one place, spelt exactly as you want them shown:

- Business name, and how it should be written (capitals, spacing, any symbols)
- What you do, in one or two sentences
- Your address or area, if customers visit you
- Opening hours
- Year started, if you want to mention it

This seems obvious, but small mistakes here end up on every page.

## 2. Contact details and WhatsApp number

Decide which phone number goes on the site. Many small businesses use one number for calls and another for WhatsApp. Pick the number that someone will actually answer during business hours.

Check that:

- The WhatsApp number is in international form, for example starting with 234 for Nigeria
- It is a number you plan to keep, since changing it means updating the site
- Your email address is one you check

Also list your social media links: Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, X, LinkedIn or whatever you use. Copy the full profile link, not just the handle.

## 3. Your logo

If you have a logo, send the best file you have. In order of preference:

1. The original design file from whoever made it
2. A PNG with a transparent background
3. A clear, large JPG

A logo cropped from a WhatsApp profile picture usually looks blurry on a website. If you do not have a logo, that is fine. A clean text version of your business name works well for many small sites.

## 4. Photos

Photos sell more than words. Gather:

- Your products, shop front, team or finished work
- Photos taken in good daylight, against a plain background where possible
- The original files, not screenshots or images saved from WhatsApp, which are compressed

Ten good photos are better than fifty average ones. Only use photos you took or have the right to use. Pictures copied from someone else's page can cause problems later.

## 5. The text for each section

You do not need to be a writer. Short and clear beats long and fancy. Prepare:

- **About:** who you are and why customers should trust you, in a short paragraph
- **Services or products:** a list, with a line or two on each, and prices if you want to show them
- **Anything customers always ask:** delivery areas, payment methods, how to book

A useful trick: look at the questions you answer most often in DMs. Those answers belong on your website.

## 6. The look you like

Have a rough idea of style and colours before the first draft. Look at a few websites you like and note what you like about them. Our [examples page](https://elevven11studio.github.io/examples/) shows sites for restaurants, fashion brands, real estate and more. If your brand already has colours, note them down.

## 7. Your domain decision

A domain is your web address, like `yourbusiness.com.ng`. You have two choices:

- **Use the free default address.** Eligible sites hosted on GitHub Pages get a free address. It works, but it is longer and carries the host's name.
- **Use your own domain.** It looks more professional and is easier to remember. Registration is a yearly cost you pay to a registrar.

If you want your own domain, check whether the name you want is available, and make sure it is registered in your name, not the designer's. Our guide on [connecting a custom domain](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/connect-a-custom-domain-to-your-website/) explains the steps.

## 8. Your budget and package

Know roughly how much you want to spend and what the site must do. A one-page site with your services and a WhatsApp button costs less than a store with 30 products. Read [how much a website costs in Nigeria](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/how-much-does-a-website-cost-in-nigeria/) before comparing quotes.
