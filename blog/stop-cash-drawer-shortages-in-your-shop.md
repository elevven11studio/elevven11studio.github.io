# How to Stop Cash Drawer Shortages in Your Shop

Simple daily habits that stop cash going missing from your till, and how a POS app with shifts and an activity log makes shortages easy to trace.

You close the shop, count the cash and it is NGN 3,500 short. Nobody knows why. It happens again next week. Most shop owners either shrug it off or accuse someone without proof. Neither fixes the problem.

Shortages come from three places: honest mistakes, sloppy records and theft. Good habits reduce all three, and they make it much easier to tell which one you are dealing with.

## Start every day with a float

A float is a fixed amount of cash in the drawer at opening, so the cashier can give change. Decide on one amount and use it every day.

- Count the float in front of the cashier who is taking over.
- Write it down, with the time and both names.
- Keep the float in small notes and coins, since that is what change needs.

Without a fixed float, you cannot know what the drawer should hold at closing.

## Know what "expected cash" really means

Expected cash is not just float plus sales. It is:

1. The opening float
2. Plus cash sales
3. Minus cash refunds
4. Minus cash paid out of the till for expenses, like fuel or a delivery boy
5. Plus or minus any other cash put in or taken out

Many "shortages" are really unrecorded payouts. Someone took NGN 2,000 for diesel and did not write it down. Make it a rule: no cash leaves the drawer without a record.

## Count at every handover

If two cashiers share one drawer through the day, a shortage cannot be traced to either of them. Close the count whenever the person at the till changes.

- One person, one drawer, one shift.
- Count at the end of each shift, not just at closing.
- Record the difference, even when it is small. Small differences that repeat are a pattern.

## Control voids, refunds and discounts

The most common way cash disappears is not someone reaching into the drawer. It is a sale that gets cancelled after the customer has paid and left. The cash stays, the record disappears, and the cashier pockets the difference.

Protect against this:

- **Voids and refunds need a reason.** Every one, every time.
- **Big discounts need approval.** Set a limit a cashier can give on their own.
- **Watch for "no sale" drawer opens.** A drawer opened without a sale should be rare.
- **Check receipt reprints.** Reprinting an old receipt can be used to fake a refund.

## Review, but ask before you accuse

Once a week, look at who voided what, who refunded what and whose shifts ran short. One void means nothing. Ten voids by the same person on Saturday evenings is worth a conversation. The records show what happened, not why, so ask first.

## How Counterbook helps

[Counterbook](https://elevven11studio.github.io/counterbook/), our free point of sale app for Windows, has these habits built in.

### Shifts and the drawer

A shift opens with the float in the drawer. Cash sales, refunds, till expenses, pay-ins and pay-outs all move the expected cash as they happen. When the cashier closes the shift, they count the drawer and Counterbook shows the expected amount and the difference. Shortages and overages are recorded, not hidden.

When you pay an expense in cash during a shift, you can mark it as taken from the till, so the count stays right.

### Staff PINs and roles

Each person gets their own PIN and a role, so every action is tied to a name. A Cashier can sell, take credit sales, manage customers and run their own shift, but does not see reports. An Accountant can see reports, expenses and the activity log. Voids, refunds, discounts and price changes need permission and a reason.

Five wrong PINs lock that person out for 30 seconds, then longer each time, up to 15 minutes. The [Counterbook docs](https://elevven11studio.github.io/counterbook/docs/) recommend 6-digit PINs for owners and managers, and deactivating people the day they leave.

### The activity log and its watch list

Owners, managers and accountants can open the activity log. Its watch list collects the actions worth checking:

| On the watch list | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Voids and refunds | Cancelled sales are the easiest way to hide taken cash |
| Large discounts | A friend's "discount" is lost money |
| Price changes | A price dropped just before a sale |
| Stock losses | Goods leaving without a sale |
| No-sale drawer opens | Cash access with no record |
| Repeated wrong PINs | Someone trying another person's login |
| Receipt reprints | Old receipts used to fake refunds |

You can also see activity by person, so a pattern stands out quickly.

### Tamper detection

Log entries are chained together. If anyone changes or deletes an old entry, the change is detected and reported. Entries cannot be edited or deleted in the app at all.

## A simple routine to start this week

1. Pick a fixed float and count it at opening.
2. Give every staff member their own login, never a shared one.
3. Close a shift every time the person at the till changes.
4. Write down every cash payout from the drawer.
5. Check voids, refunds and shortages by person once a week.
