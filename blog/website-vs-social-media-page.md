# Website vs Social Media Page: Does Your Business Need Both?

Why a business with an active Instagram or Facebook page still needs its own website, and how the two work together to bring in customers.

Many small businesses in Nigeria run entirely on Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp. It works. Customers find you, send a DM and pay by transfer. So why spend money on a website?

Because a social media page and a website do different jobs. One is good at getting attention. The other is good at answering questions and building trust. Most businesses do best with both.

## What your social media page is good at

Social media is where people scroll. It is a strong place to:

- Show new stock, new work and behind-the-scenes posts
- Reach people who did not know you existed
- Chat with customers in comments and DMs
- Run short promotions that people see the same day

Keep doing all of this. A website does not replace it.

## Where a social media page falls short

### You do not own it

Your page lives on someone else's platform, under their rules. If your account is hacked, reported or locked, your customers lose the only place they knew to find you. A website on your own address stays yours.

### Your information gets buried

The price list you posted last month is now under forty other posts. Your opening hours are in a story that disappeared. A new customer has to scroll, guess or send a DM asking the same questions you answered last week.

A website keeps the basics in one fixed place: what you sell, what it costs, where you are and how to reach you.

### Not everyone uses the same app

Some of your customers are on Instagram, some only on Facebook, and some use neither. A website opens in any browser on any phone, with no login and no app to install.

### Google search shows websites

When someone searches "bakery in Wuse 2" or "plumber in Lekki", they are already looking to buy. A website and a [Google Business Profile](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/google-business-profile-setup-guide/) give you a better chance of showing up for those searches than a social page alone.

### It looks more established

Fair or not, a business with its own site looks more permanent. For bigger orders, corporate clients or customers who have been scammed before, that matters. A link to `yourbusiness.com.ng` reads differently from a link to a profile.

## How the two work together

Think of social media as the shop window and your website as the counter where people get answers. They should point to each other.

| Job | Social media page | Your website |
|---|---|---|
| Getting noticed | Strong | Weak on its own |
| Daily updates and chat | Strong | Not needed |
| Prices, services and location in one place | Hard to keep tidy | Strong |
| Showing up on Google | Limited | Strong |
| Staying under your control | No | Yes |

A simple setup that works for most small businesses:

1. Put your website link in your Instagram and Facebook bio.
2. Put your social links and a WhatsApp button on your website.
3. When someone DMs a common question, reply with a link to the right page.
4. Print one QR code that opens your site, so flyers and signs send people to the full picture. Our [QR code guide](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/qr-codes-for-small-business/) covers print tips.

Now each channel does what it is good at. Social media brings people in. The website answers their questions. WhatsApp closes the sale.
