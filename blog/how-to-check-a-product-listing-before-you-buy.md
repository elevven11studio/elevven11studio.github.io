# How to Check a Product Listing Before You Buy

A practical checklist for online shopping: test the discount, read reviews properly, check the seller, and find the real total before you pay.

A product page is written to sell. That does not make it dishonest, but it does mean the most flattering numbers are the biggest ones on the page. A few minutes of checking before you pay can save you a return, a wasted delivery fee or a product that is not what you thought.

Here is what to look at, in order.

## 1. Check that the discount adds up

A listing shows "40% off", a was-price of NGN 25,000 and a price of NGN 17,500. Do the sum:

- The saving is 25,000 minus 17,500, which is NGN 7,500.
- 7,500 divided by 25,000 is 0.30, so the real discount is **30%**, not 40%.

A mismatch like that is not proof of fraud. The was-price might be out of date. But it tells you the headline number is not reliable, so judge the price on its own. And remember a big discount on a price nobody ever paid is not a saving.

## 2. Weigh the rating by the number of reviews

A 4.9 rating from 7 buyers tells you much less than a 4.4 rating from 1,200. A small number of reviews can swing on one or two people.

When you read reviews:

- **Start with the 2 and 3 star ones.** They tend to be the most specific about real problems.
- **Look for repeated complaints.** One person saying the charger broke is bad luck. Ten people saying it is a pattern.
- **Be wary of many short, similar reviews posted close together**, but do not treat that as proof. Real buyers write short reviews in bursts too.

## 3. Find out who is selling it

On marketplaces like Jumia, Konga, Amazon or AliExpress, the store and the seller are often different. Check:

- Whether it is sold by the platform or by a third-party seller.
- The seller's own rating and how long they have been selling, where shown.
- Where the item ships from, which affects delivery time and returns.

## 4. Work out the real total

The price on the page is rarely what leaves your account. Add the delivery charge, and check whether it changes by location. A NGN 17,500 item with NGN 3,500 delivery costs NGN 21,000, which may beat a cheaper listing with higher delivery, or may not.

Then check the delivery estimate, the return window and the warranty. If you cannot find them, do not assume they do not exist. Many stores keep policies on a separate page. Find that page before you pay.

## 5. Read the specifications against the title

Listings often contradict themselves. The title says 128 GB and the spec table says 64 GB. The photos show three items and the description says one. When the page disagrees with itself, ask the seller in writing before buying, so you have the answer on record.

## 6. Make sure the shop itself is real

Everything above assumes you are on a genuine store. If you arrived from an advert or a link in a message, check the web address first. Our guide on [how to spot a phishing website](https://elevven11studio.github.io/blog/how-to-spot-a-phishing-website/) covers what to look for.

## Doing the checks with ShopInspect

[ShopInspect](https://elevven11studio.github.io/shopinspect/) is a free Chrome extension we built to run this checklist on the listing in front of you. Open a product page and click the toolbar button, right-click, or press `Ctrl+Shift+Y`.

It reports on price, reviews, seller, product details, delivery and returns, value and listing quality. It checks whether an advertised discount matches the two prices shown, weighs the rating by how many buyers stand behind it, and shows what the visible reviews praise and complain about. Every finding links to where it came from on the page.

The result is a verdict from Excellent Buy to Poor Value, or Not Enough Information when the listing states too little. If less than 40% of what it looks for is on the page, it gives no verdict at all.

It is also clear about its limits. It cannot tell you the price is good compared to other stores, and it does not check whether a site is a scam. It works on most stores, with extra support for marketplaces including Jumia and Konga. Everything runs in your browser and there is no shopping history.

## The short checklist

Before you pay, confirm the discount maths, read the middle reviews, know who the seller is, add delivery to the price, and find the return policy. If any of those is unclear, ask first or shop elsewhere.
