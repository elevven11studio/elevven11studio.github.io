Property is a trust business. A buyer may be about to spend years of savings, and a renter may be paying a full year upfront. Before they call you, they look you up to answer one question: is this agent real, and are these listings real?

Your website is where you answer that. It does not need to be big. It needs to be current, specific and easy to act on.

## Listings come first

People visit an agent's site to see property. Put listings at the top of the page, before your story.

Each listing should show, at a glance:

- **What it is and where.** "3-bed flat, Ikoyi" is better than "Luxury apartment".
- **Price, and whether it is for sale or rent.** For rentals, say if the price is per year.
- **The key facts.** Bedrooms, bathrooms, size in square metres for land or offices, and features buyers in your area care about, such as a boys' quarters, serviced estate or steady power.
- **Clear photos.** Daylight, tidy rooms, and the outside of the building. Photos of the actual property, not similar ones.
- **Document status where relevant.** For land especially, buyers want to know what title documents exist.

Then add one way to act on it: "Book a viewing" or "Ask about this property".

## Keep listings fresh or take them down

Nothing hurts an agent's site more than a property that sold months ago still showing as available. A buyer who calls about it feels baited, even if you meant no harm.

Decide how you will handle this before the site goes live:

1. Show a small number of featured listings rather than everything you have.
2. Mark sold or let properties clearly, or remove them.
3. Set a regular time to send updates, for example every two weeks.

Changing text and replacing images are listed as free updates during your update window, so ask up front how listing changes will be handled. A full property database with search filters is a different kind of website. It needs a database, and the [pricing page](/pricing/) lists database-backed functionality as quoted separately. For most independent agents and small agencies, a handful of well-kept featured listings works better anyway.

## Answer the trust questions up front

Buyers and renters ask the same questions every time. Answer them on the site:

- **Do you charge for viewings?** Say clearly what is free and when fees apply.
- **Are documents checked?** If you verify title documents before listing, say so.
- **Do you handle rentals as well as sales?**
- **When can I view?** Your viewing days and hours.
- **What if I live abroad?** If you can send walkthrough videos to buyers in another state or country, say so. Diaspora buyers look for this.

A short "why work with us" section helps too, as long as it is specific. "Over a decade handling first-time buyer paperwork and commercial leases" says more than "trusted and reliable".

## Common mistakes

- **Prices hidden behind "call for price" on every listing.** Some high-value properties call for discretion, but if every listing hides the price, visitors leave to find an agent who shows them.
- **Borrowed photos.** If a buyer recognises a photo from another agent's post, your credibility is gone.
- **Only a phone number.** Many enquiries now start on WhatsApp. Give both.
- **No physical address or registration details.** Show where your office is. If you belong to a professional body, say which one.

## Three styles of agency

Our real estate demos cover three common types:

- [Prime Realty Advisors](/examples/real-estate/) is a general residential and commercial agency in Lagos: featured listings with prices and key facts, a "why work with us" section, a "just listed" strip, viewing hours with a note on walkthrough videos, common questions about fees, documents and rentals, and a "Speak With an Agent" button.
- [Halima Yusuf Properties](/examples/real-estate-2/) is a luxury advisory in Abuja, with a small "current collection", a short section on discretion, and a "Request an Introduction" button for private consultations.
- [UrbanNest Realty](/examples/real-estate-3/) is rental-heavy and fast-moving, opening with "new this week" listings, mostly yearly rents, and a simple "Get Started" call to action.

The structure is similar, but the tone differs. A luxury agent shows fewer properties and promises privacy. A rental agent shows more and promises speed.
