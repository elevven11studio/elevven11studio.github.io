A QR code saves your customer from typing. Instead of copying a long web address or a phone number off a flyer, they point their camera and tap. For a small business, that small saving is often the difference between someone getting in touch and someone forgetting.

Here are the uses that work best, and how to print a code so it scans first time.

## What a QR code can hold

A QR code is a grid of squares that stores text in a pattern a phone camera can read. That text can be:

- **A link**, which opens a web page.
- **Plain text**, which shows on the screen.
- **An email address**, with an optional subject and message.
- **A phone number**, ready to call.
- **Wi-Fi details**, so the phone can join the network.

A basic code holds the text directly. There is no tracking and nothing to expire, so it keeps working as long as the thing it points to still exists.

## Practical uses

### A menu on the table

Restaurants, bukas and cafes can put the menu on a web page and print a code for each table. When prices change, you update the page instead of reprinting menus. Keep a few printed menus for customers who prefer paper.

### Chat on WhatsApp

A WhatsApp link opens a chat with your business in one tap. The format is `https://wa.me/` followed by your number in international format, without the plus sign or leading zero, such as `https://wa.me/2348012345678`. Put that link in a code on your shop window, delivery bags or business card. If you sell through WhatsApp, see [how to sell on WhatsApp with your own online store](/blog/sell-on-whatsapp-with-your-own-online-store/).

### Your website

A code on a flyer or poster that opens your website gives people your full price list, photos and location, more than any flyer can hold.

### Payment details on flyers and invoices

Use the text type to hold your bank name, account number and account name. A customer who scans it can copy the details without typos. Do the same at the bottom of an invoice, next to the written details.

### Wi-Fi for customers

A Wi-Fi code on the counter lets customers join your guest network without you reading out the password every time.

### Reviews

Link a code to your Google Business Profile so happy customers can leave a review on the spot. Our [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/google-business-profile-setup-guide/) covers setting that up.

## Printing tips

Most QR codes that fail are printed badly, not made badly.

1. **Size.** Print it at least 2 cm wide for close scanning, like a flyer held in the hand. Go much bigger for posters or signs read from across a room.
2. **Contrast.** Use a dark code on a light background. Black on white is safest. Light codes on dark backgrounds fail on many scanners.
3. **Margin.** Leave a blank border around the code. Text or patterns right up against the edge confuse scanners.
4. **Keep the content short.** A long link or a paragraph of text makes a denser code that needs to be printed larger.
5. **Choose the right file.** SVG stays sharp at any size, so use it for banners and signs. PNG is fine for documents and screens.
6. **Error correction.** Higher levels let a code scan even when part of it is dirty or covered, but make it denser. Medium suits most uses.
7. **Test before you print in bulk.** Scan it with two or three different phones, from the distance people will actually stand.

Also tell people what they will get. "Scan for our menu" or "Scan to chat on WhatsApp" next to the code makes far more people scan it than a bare square.
