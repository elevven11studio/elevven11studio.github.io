A school website has two audiences, and they want different things. Parents who are choosing a school want to know if their child will be safe, taught well and worth the fees. Parents whose children are already enrolled want term dates and a phone number. A good school site serves the first group without making the second dig.

This post is general website guidance. It is not advice on regulations, accreditation or admissions policy, so check what your school board or regulator expects you to publish.

## Answer the parent's questions in order

A parent comparing schools tends to move through the same questions. Arrange your page in roughly the same order.

1. **What kind of school is this?** Nursery, primary, secondary, exam prep or skills training, and for which ages. Say it in the first line.
2. **What will my child do here?** Your classes or programmes, each with a sentence on what it covers.
3. **Who is in charge?** A short note from the head or director, with their name.
4. **When does term run?** Resumption dates, breaks and holiday programmes.
5. **How do we join?** The admissions steps, from first contact to a confirmed place.
6. **How are fees paid?** Whether fees are termly, by transfer, and whether payment plans exist.
7. **Can I visit?** A button to book a tour or an assessment.

If a parent can get through all seven on a phone in a few minutes, they will call you with fewer, better questions.

## Make admissions a clear path

Admissions is the part that turns interest into enrolment, and it is often the vaguest section on a school site. Spell out the steps. For example: book an assessment, meet the class teacher, receive an offer within a set time. If places are limited, say so honestly.

Then give one clear action: "Schedule a tour", "Book a free assessment" or "Apply now". On a simple site, that button opens WhatsApp or a contact form so the parent can reach the admissions desk directly.

## Show the people, carefully

Parents want to see real classrooms and real staff. Photos of your building, your teachers and school events build trust faster than stock images.

Be careful with photos of children. Only use images you have clear permission from parents to publish, and avoid showing names alongside faces. When in doubt, use photos of spaces and activities where children are not identifiable.

## Different schools, different priorities

The same structure works for most schools, but the emphasis changes. Our three school demos show this:

- [Brightpath Tutorial Centre](/examples/school/) is exam prep. It leads with JAMB and WAEC/NECO programmes and one-on-one tutoring, then a quote from the director, term dates, the admissions process, common questions and a "Book a Free Assessment" button.
- [Kiddies Corner](/examples/school-2/) is a nursery and primary school. It leads with classes by age, from creche to Primary 6, a note from the principal, and a "Schedule a Tour" button, because parents of young children usually want to see the place first.
- [TechSkills Academy](/examples/school-3/) is a coding bootcamp for adults. It lists tracks with their length in weeks and pushes "Apply for the Next Cohort", with a note on payment plans and scholarships.

A nursery sells safety and warmth. A tutorial centre sells results. A training academy sells skills that lead to work.

## Mistakes that worry parents

- **Last session's dates.** Old term dates suggest nobody updates anything else either.
- **Claims you cannot back up.** "Best school in the state" or "100% pass rate" invites doubt. If you publish results, keep them accurate and explain what they cover.
- **No fee information at all.** You do not have to publish exact fees, but saying how and when fees are paid answers a question every parent has.
- **Hard-to-find contact details.** A phone number and WhatsApp button on every screen.

## What a website is not

A school website and a school management system are different things. Our websites are static, which keeps them fast and free of monthly hosting fees. That means they do not include:

- Parent or student logins
- Result checking or report cards
- Online fee payments
- Attendance or records

Those need a database and authentication, which the [pricing page](/pricing/) lists as quoted separately. Many schools keep a separate portal for these and link to it from the website. That way the public site stays simple and the private data stays in a system built for it.

## Plan for each new term

Term dates and admissions notices change every term. Those count as text changes, which are free during your update window. After that, someone at the school should own the job of sending updates.
