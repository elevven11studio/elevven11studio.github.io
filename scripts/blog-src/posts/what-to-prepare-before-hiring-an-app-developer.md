Most app projects that go over budget do not fail because of bad code. They fail because nobody agreed on what the app should do before work started. Every new idea halfway through costs time and money.

You do not need technical knowledge to prepare well. You need clear answers to a few questions. This checklist covers what to sort out before you contact any developer, us included.

## 1. Know what the app is for

Write one or two sentences that answer: what problem does this app solve, and for whom?

- "Customers can see our menu, order and track delivery."
- "Our field staff can record site visits with photos, even without network."

If you cannot write this sentence, the app is not ready to quote. Also ask yourself honestly whether you need an app at all. A good mobile website is often cheaper and enough. See [one-page vs multi-page website](/blog/one-page-vs-multi-page-website/) if a website might do the job.

## 2. Know who will use it

List the kinds of people who will open the app:

- Your customers?
- Your staff?
- You, as the admin?

Each group usually needs its own screens. An app for customers and an admin panel for you are two pieces of work.

## 3. List the features, then cut the list

Write every feature you want. Then mark each one as **must have** for launch or **later**. Features that usually add the most cost include:

- User accounts and logins
- Payments inside the app
- A server or database behind it
- Push notifications
- Working offline and syncing later
- Maps, chat or live tracking
- An admin panel to manage content

A small first version that launches is worth more than a big one that never does. You can add features after launch.

## 4. Decide on Android, iPhone or both

In Nigeria, most of your users may be on Android, but business customers often use iPhones. Frameworks like Flutter build both from one codebase, so supporting both costs less than two separate apps. Read [why one Flutter codebase covers Android and iOS](/blog/flutter-app-one-codebase-android-ios/) for more.

Launching on one store first is still a valid way to save money.

## 5. Gather your content and branding

Developers move faster when you have these ready:

- Logo and brand colours
- Product names, prices, photos and descriptions
- Text for key screens, like your About and Terms
- An existing website or system the app should connect to

Content you can already supply keeps the quote down.

## 6. Find two or three apps you like

Note apps that do something close to what you want. Write what you like about each one, such as the checkout flow or how the menu looks. This gives a developer a clear picture faster than any long description.

## 7. Plan for the costs after the build

The build is not the only cost. Plan for:

| Cost | How it is billed |
|---|---|
| Google Play developer registration | One-off fee |
| Apple Developer Program | Yearly fee |
| Server, database, notifications | Ongoing, if your app needs them |
| Payment provider fees | Per transaction |
| New features after launch | Quoted separately |

Ask any developer to list these before you agree.

## 8. Open your own store accounts

Your app should be published under store accounts in your business name, not the developer's. If the developer owns the account, they own your listing. Start creating your Google Play and Apple accounts early, since verification can take time.

## 9. Set a realistic timeline

An app takes longer than a website. Apple and Google also review apps before they go live, and that adds time you cannot control. If you have a launch date, tell the developer at the start.

## 10. Ask the right questions

Before you pay a deposit, ask:

1. Is the price fixed in writing, or can it change?
2. Will I see and approve the screens before they are built?
3. Can I install a test version on my phone before launch?
4. What happens if something breaks after launch?
5. Who owns the code and the store listing?

## The short version

Know what the app is for, who uses it and which features must be in the first version. Gather your content, plan for store and server costs, and get a fixed price in writing. Do that and any good developer can give you an honest quote.
