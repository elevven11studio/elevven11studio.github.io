An invoice is a request for payment. A clear one gets paid faster, avoids "what is this for?" phone calls, and gives you a record you can find later when you check your income or deal with tax.

It does not need fancy software. It needs the right details in a clear order.

## Quote, invoice or receipt?

These three are often confused:

- **A quote** is an estimate you send before the work, so the customer can agree to a price.
- **An invoice** asks for payment after the work is done or the goods are delivered.
- **A receipt** confirms that payment has been made.

If a customer pays in advance, you can still send an invoice first and a receipt once the money arrives.

## What every invoice must include

1. **Your business name, address and contact details.** Phone and email at least.
2. **The customer's name and address.** For a company, use its registered name.
3. **A unique invoice number.** Such as INV-001, then INV-002.
4. **The invoice date and a due date.**
5. **Each item or service** with its quantity, price and line total.
6. **Any discount and tax**, shown separately from the subtotal.
7. **The total due.**
8. **How to pay:** bank name, account number and account name.
9. **Your tax number**, if you are registered and the rules require it.

Leave out any of the first eight and you are likely to get a question instead of a payment.

## Numbering invoices

Use a simple running sequence and never reuse a number. INV-001, INV-002, INV-003 is enough. Some businesses add the year, such as 2026-001, which makes yearly records easier to sort.

Keep a list of numbers you have issued so none is skipped or repeated. If an invoice is cancelled, mark it cancelled rather than using its number again.

## How the totals work

Work out the figures in this order:

1. **Line total** = quantity x price, for each item.
2. **Subtotal** = all line totals added together.
3. **Discount** = subtotal x discount % / 100.
4. **Tax** = (subtotal minus discount) x tax % / 100.
5. **Total** = subtotal minus discount plus tax.

Tax is charged on what remains after the discount, not on the full subtotal.

### Worked example

A tailor invoices a customer for three shirts at NGN 15,000 each and one pair of trousers at NGN 20,000, with a 10% discount and 7.5% VAT.

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| 3 shirts x NGN 15,000 | NGN 45,000 |
| 1 trousers x NGN 20,000 | NGN 20,000 |
| Subtotal | NGN 65,000 |
| Discount (10%) | minus NGN 6,500 |
| VAT (7.5% of NGN 58,500) | NGN 4,387.50 |
| **Total due** | **NGN 62,887.50** |

Check: 65,000 minus 6,500 is 58,500. 7.5% of 58,500 is 4,387.50. And 58,500 plus 4,387.50 is 62,887.50.

If you need more on the tax line, see [how to calculate VAT in Nigeria](/blog/how-to-calculate-vat-in-nigeria/).

## Habits that get you paid faster

- **Send it straight away.** The day the work is done or agreed, not at the end of the month.
- **Set a real due date.** "Pay within 7 days" is clearer than "pay soon".
- **Describe items specifically.** "Logo design, 3 concepts and 2 revisions" beats "design work".
- **Put payment details on the invoice itself**, so the customer does not have to ask for your account number.
- **Ask for a deposit on large jobs.** Invoice part up front and the balance on delivery.
- **Keep a copy of every invoice**, and note when each one is paid.

## Is it a legal tax invoice?

The list above covers what most invoices need. If you are registered for VAT, the tax rules may ask for extra details, such as your tax number. Requirements differ and change, so check with the tax authority or an accountant.
