Most people who visit a church website have never been to that church. They moved to a new area, got invited by a colleague, or are looking for somewhere to take their children. They want to know three things before Sunday: when to come, where to go, and what to expect when they walk in.

Members already know all of that. So a church website is mostly for the person who has not arrived yet.

## Start with the newcomer

Picture someone reading your site on a phone on Saturday night. Every section should answer a question they would feel awkward asking out loud.

- **What time is service, and how long does it run?** Put service times near the top, not on a separate page three taps away.
- **Where exactly are you?** A street address, a nearby landmark and a map link. "Opposite the filling station" helps more than a postcode.
- **What do I wear? Can I bring my kids?** A short line on dress and children's church settles a lot of nerves.
- **Will I be asked for money?** Many visitors worry about this. Saying plainly that giving is voluntary removes a reason not to come.
- **Who do I talk to?** A name and a way to send a message before the visit.

## The sections that earn their place

### Service times and the weekly rhythm

Sunday times first, then the rest of the week: Bible study, youth fellowship, prayer meetings. Add who each one is for, for example "ages 13 to 24" for youth. Visitors use this to find a way in that suits them, and some will try a midweek meeting before a Sunday.

### A word from the pastor

Two or three sentences in the pastor's own voice, with a name and a photo. This is often the part that makes a stranger feel they would be welcome. Keep it warm and short. A long doctrinal statement can live elsewhere if you need it.

### This Sunday

One block that says what is coming up: the sermon title or series, who is preaching, and anything different about the day. A site still advertising last year's convention tells visitors nobody is minding the door.

### A clear first step

"Plan your visit", "Send us a message" or "Send a prayer request". Pick one main action and put it at the top and bottom of the page. On a small church site, that button usually opens WhatsApp, which is where most people in Nigeria would rather talk anyway.

## Mistakes that keep visitors away

1. **Out-of-date times.** The single most damaging error. If someone arrives at 9am for a service that moved to 8am, they may not come back.
2. **Pages full of church language.** Terms like "altar call" or "ministration" mean nothing to an outsider. Write for someone who has never attended.
3. **Only a Facebook page.** Social posts scroll away. A website keeps the essentials in one fixed place. We compare the two in [website vs social media page](/blog/website-vs-social-media-page/).

## Ministries, NGOs and online churches

Not every ministry is a single congregation. Our church demos show three different shapes:

- [Grace Covenant Chapel](/examples/church/) is a local church: service times, a word from the pastor, this Sunday's message, the weekly schedule, common questions and a "New Here?" message button.
- [Hope Relief Foundation](/examples/church-2/) is an NGO layout: headline numbers on people and communities reached, its programmes, and a "Get Involved" section for volunteers, partners and donors.
- [Living Word International](/examples/church-3/) is media-forward: a "Watch Live" button at the top, a row for recent messages, and a prayer request button for people who follow from far away.

If most members watch online, lead with the stream link.

## What a simple site cannot do

Our websites are static, which is why they can be hosted with no monthly fee. That covers service times, sermon links, events and contact buttons. It does not cover features that need a database or a login:

- Member accounts or a member directory
- Online giving processed on the site itself
- Event registration with seat counts
- Hosting your own video stream

A static site can link to your YouTube or Facebook live stream, and it can show your church account details for people who want to give by transfer. Anything beyond that, such as payments or bookings, is quoted separately, as listed on the [pricing page](/pricing/).
