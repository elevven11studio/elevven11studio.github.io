"Updates" is one of the most argued-about words in web design. A business owner asks for "a small update", the designer sends a quote, and both feel the other is being unreasonable. Most of these arguments come from never agreeing what the word means. This guide sets out a clear line between an update and new work, so you know what to expect from any designer.

## Why the line matters

Changing a phone number takes a few minutes. Adding a booking system can take days. Both get called "updates" in conversation.

If a designer promises "free updates" without defining them, one of two things happens. Either they end up doing unpaid work they never planned for, or you get a bill you did not expect. A written policy protects both sides.

## Updates vs new work

The simplest test is this: **are you changing something that exists, or adding something that does not?**

Changing existing content is an update. Adding new pages, features or systems is new work.

Here is how we draw the line at Elevven11 Studio. It is the same list that appears on our [pricing page](/pricing/).

| Counts as a free update | Quoted separately |
|---|---|
| Changing text | New pages |
| Replacing images | Major redesign |
| Updating phone number | New functionality |
| Updating WhatsApp number | Online payments and stock tracking |
| Updating business information | Booking systems |
| Updating social links | Authentication (logins) |
| Correcting small content issues | Database-backed functionality |
| | Major structural changes |

A few examples to make it concrete:

- New prices on your services list: **free update.**
- A new photo of your shop: **free update.**
- A typo on the About section: **free update.**
- A new "Gallery" page: **new work.**
- A form where customers book appointments: **new work.**
- Moving from one page to several pages: **new work.**

## How to get the most from your update period

The first weeks after launch are when you notice what needs changing. Use that window well:

1. **Check every page on your phone.** Most of your visitors will see it there.
2. **Ask two or three customers to look.** They spot confusing wording you have stopped noticing.
3. **Send changes in one list.** A single clear message with every change is faster to handle than ten separate ones.
4. **Be specific.** "Change the price of braids from 15,000 to 18,000" is better than "update the prices".
5. **Don't wait until the last day.** Send your list early so there is time for a second round if needed.

## Planning for changes after the period ends

Your business will change after the free period. Prices go up, numbers change, staff move on. Plan for it:

- **Keep your content simple.** A site that lists services with "from" prices needs fewer edits than one with every price spelled out.
- **Put fast-changing things on social media.** Daily specials or limited stock work better on your WhatsApp status, with the website linking to it.
- **Bundle changes.** Collect small edits and request them together rather than one at a time.

## Questions to ask any designer

Whoever builds your site, get clear answers to these before you pay:

1. What exactly counts as a free update?
2. How long do free updates last, and when does the clock start?
3. What does a change cost after that period?
4. How do I send a change request, and how quickly is it done?
