Every business has a number of sales below which it loses money and above which it starts to earn. That number is the break-even point. Knowing it turns a vague hope ("I think we are doing fine") into a target you can check every week.

You only need three figures to find it.

## The three numbers you need

1. **Fixed costs.** Costs that stay the same whether you sell one item or a thousand: rent, salaries, subscriptions, loan repayments. Work them out for a period, usually a month.
2. **Variable cost per unit.** What it costs you to make or buy one more unit: materials, packaging, delivery, sales commission.
3. **Selling price per unit.** What the customer pays for one.

## The formula

Each sale brings in its price, but part of that pays for the unit itself. What is left goes towards your fixed costs.

- **Profit per unit** = selling price minus variable cost
- **Break-even units** = fixed costs / profit per unit, rounded up
- **Break-even revenue** = break-even units x selling price

You round up because you cannot sell part of a unit. If the answer is 333.3, you need 334 sales.

## Worked example: a small bakery

A bakery sells loaves of bread. Its monthly fixed costs are:

| Cost | Per month |
|---|---|
| Rent | NGN 60,000 |
| Staff | NGN 100,000 |
| Power and other running costs | NGN 40,000 |
| **Total fixed costs** | **NGN 200,000** |

Each loaf costs NGN 700 in flour, sugar, yeast and wrapping, and sells for NGN 1,200.

1. Profit per loaf is 1,200 minus 700, which is **NGN 500**.
2. Break-even units are 200,000 / 500, which is **400 loaves**.
3. Break-even revenue is 400 x 1,200, which is **NGN 480,000**.

So the bakery must sell 400 loaves a month just to cover its costs. Over 30 days that is 13.3 a day, so 14 loaves a day to be safe. Loaf number 401 is the first one that makes a profit.

## Adding a profit target

Breaking even is not the goal, it is the floor. To plan for a profit, add your target to the fixed costs:

- **Units for a target** = (fixed costs + target profit) / profit per unit

If the bakery wants NGN 100,000 profit a month:

- (200,000 + 100,000) / 500 is **600 loaves**, or NGN 720,000 in sales.

That is 20 loaves a day. Now the owner can ask a useful question: can we really sell 20 a day?

## What changes the number

Small changes to price or cost move the break-even point a lot.

| Change | Profit per loaf | Break-even |
|---|---|---|
| No change | NGN 500 | 400 loaves |
| Price up to NGN 1,300 | NGN 600 | 334 loaves |
| Flour pushes cost to NGN 800 | NGN 400 | 500 loaves |

A NGN 100 rise in the cost of each loaf means 100 more loaves a month just to stand still. That is why it pays to recheck the numbers whenever suppliers raise prices. At NGN 1,300, the calculation is 200,000 / 600, which is 333.3, rounded up to 334.

## When there is no break-even point

If the selling price is not higher than the variable cost, every sale loses money. Selling more only makes the loss bigger, so there is no break-even point at all. The fix is a higher price or a lower unit cost, not more sales. Our guide to [profit margin vs markup](/blog/profit-margin-vs-markup/) helps with setting that price.

## Tips for using it

- **Use a consistent period.** If fixed costs are monthly, the result is sales per month.
- **Be honest about fixed costs.** Include your own salary if you take one, or the business looks healthier than it is.
- **For services, treat one job or one hour as a unit**, with its direct cost as the variable cost.
- **Count every variable cost.** Delivery and packaging per item add up quickly.
