When someone near you searches "pharmacy near me" or "barber in Lekki", Google often shows a map with a few businesses pinned on it before any website appears. Those listings come from Google Business Profile. If your business is not there, you are missing customers who were ready to visit. This guide explains what the profile does, what to prepare, and how to keep it useful.

## What a Google Business Profile is

A Google Business Profile is a free listing that Google shows on Search and Maps. It can include:

- Your business name and category
- Your address on the map, or the areas you serve
- Phone number and website link
- Opening hours
- Photos of your shop, products or work
- Customer reviews and your replies

People can call you, get directions or open your website straight from the listing, often without visiting any other page.

## Why it matters for a small business

**It is where local searches end up.** A lot of "near me" searches are answered by the map results, not by websites. A business with a complete profile can show up there even without a big online presence.

**It builds trust before the first call.** Photos, hours and reviews answer the questions customers have before they come to you.

**It makes you easy to find.** Customers who heard about you from a friend can search your name and get directions straight to your door.

**It works with your website.** Your profile links to your site, and your site confirms the details on your profile. Each makes the other more believable.

## What to prepare before you start

Gather these first. Having them ready makes setup much quicker.

1. **Your exact business name.** Use the name on your signboard and documents. Do not stuff keywords into it, like "Ada's Salon Best Hair Lagos". Google can suspend listings for this.
2. **Your category.** Pick the closest match, like "Barber shop" or "Bakery". The main category affects which searches you appear in.
3. **Address or service area.** If customers come to you, use your full street address. If you go to them, like a plumber or caterer, you can list the areas you serve and hide your address.
4. **Phone number.** One that is answered during business hours. A WhatsApp Business number works if you also take calls on it.
5. **Opening hours.** Real hours, including days you close early.
6. **Website link.** Your own site, not just a social media page, if you have one.
7. **Photos.** Your shopfront, so people recognise it on arrival, plus the inside and your products or work. Clear daylight photos from a phone are fine.
8. **A short description.** Two or three sentences on what you do and who you serve.

## Verification

Google asks you to verify that you run the business before your profile goes fully live. The method depends on your business and location, and Google tells you which options are available when you set up. It can take a few days, so start early.

Use the same name, address and phone number everywhere: on your profile, your website, your social pages and your flyers. Matching details help Google trust the listing.

## Keeping the profile working

Setup is the start. A profile that is kept up to date tends to do better than one left alone.

- **Ask happy customers for reviews.** Send them the review link after a good job. Most people will leave one if you ask at the right moment.
- **Reply to every review.** Thank people for good ones. Answer bad ones calmly and briefly. Future customers read your replies.
- **Update holiday hours.** Nothing loses a customer faster than travelling to a shop that the map said was open.
- **Add new photos now and then.** New products, a refreshed shop, recent work.
- **Check your details.** Google and the public can suggest edits to listings, so look at your profile from time to time to make sure it is still correct.
