One of the first decisions in any website project is how many pages you need. More pages are not automatically better. A barber with five services and a WhatsApp number does not need the same site as a consultancy with six service lines. Here is how to tell which camp you are in.

## What a one-page website is

A one-page site puts everything on a single long page. Visitors scroll through sections: who you are, what you offer, and how to reach you. A menu at the top jumps to each section.

It works well when your message is short and your goal is simple: get the visitor to call, send a WhatsApp message or visit your shop.

**Good fit for:**

- Barbers, stylists and beauty services
- Caterers, small kitchens and bakers
- Freelancers and personal portfolios
- Event planners and photographers just starting out
- Any business where most enquiries happen on WhatsApp

The strength of a one-page site is focus. There is nowhere to get lost, and on a phone, scrolling is natural.

## What a multi-page website is

A multi-page site splits content into separate pages, usually Home, About, Services and Contact, sometimes with more. Each page has its own address, like `yourbusiness.com/services/`.

**Good fit for:**

- Businesses with several distinct services that each need explaining
- Consultants, clinics, schools and agencies where trust depends on detail
- Businesses that want to be found on Google for different services
- Anyone who expects to add content over time

## The search engine question

This is where the two really differ. Google ranks pages, not websites. A page about "AC repair in Abuja" can rank for that search. A section halfway down a single page has a harder time.

So if customers search for specific services in your area, separate pages give each service its own chance to show up. If most of your customers come from referrals, Instagram or WhatsApp status, search matters less and a single page is often enough.

## Five questions to help you choose

1. **How much do you need to say?** If you can explain your business in a few short sections, one page is enough.
2. **How many services do you offer?** One to four simple services fit on one page. More than that, or services that need real explanation, suit separate pages.
3. **Where do your customers come from?** Social media and word of mouth favour one page. Google searches favour several.
4. **Will you add content later?** If you plan to post updates, projects or new services, a multi-page structure has room to grow.
5. **What is your budget right now?** A one-page site costs less and can still look professional.

If you answered "one page" to most of these, start there.

## Can you start small and grow later?

Yes, but plan for it. Small changes like new text, images or phone numbers count as free updates during your update period. Adding new pages counts as new work and is quoted separately. Our post on [what counts as a website update](/blog/what-counts-as-a-website-update/) explains the difference.

If you are fairly sure you will need extra pages within a few months, it is usually simpler to start with Plus than to add pages later.
