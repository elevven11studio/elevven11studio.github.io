"Write it down, I will pay on Friday." Every shop owner has heard it. Selling on credit keeps good customers loyal, but it is also how many small shops quietly run out of money. The stock is gone, the cash never comes, and the notebook with the debts is half missing.

Credit is not the problem. Unmanaged credit is. Here is how to give it without losing track.

## Decide who gets credit

Not every customer should get credit. Start with people you know:

- Regulars who have bought from you for months.
- Customers whose address or workplace you know.
- Businesses that buy from you every week, like a restaurant buying provisions.

New customers can earn credit over time. Saying "we do credit for regulars" is fair, and most people understand it.

## Set a limit for every customer

A credit limit is the most a customer can owe you at once. Without one, a debt grows one small purchase at a time until it is too big to collect.

- Start small. Raise the limit after the customer pays on time a few times.
- Base it on what they usually buy in a week or two, not what they ask for.
- Stop new credit once the limit is reached, until they pay something.

The limit also protects the customer relationship. "Your account is full, pay part and I will serve you" is easier to say than "I don't trust you."

## Keep one clear record per customer

For each customer, record:

1. Their name and phone number.
2. Every credit sale, with the date and items.
3. Every payment, with the date and amount.
4. The balance they owe now.

Keep it in one place. A debt written on a scrap of paper, a WhatsApp message and a staff member's memory is three records that will never agree.

When a customer disputes a balance, you should be able to show every sale and payment line by line. That ends most arguments quickly.

## Agree when they will pay

"Later" is not a date. Agree on one: end of the week, end of the month, or payday. Write it down.

For larger business customers, send an invoice with a due date. It looks professional and gives both sides a record. If you have not written one before, see [how to write an invoice](/blog/how-to-write-an-invoice/).

## Follow up early and politely

The longer a debt sits, the harder it is to collect. A simple routine works:

- **On the due date:** a friendly reminder by call or WhatsApp.
- **A week late:** remind again and pause new credit.
- **A month late:** ask for part payment and agree on a plan for the rest.

Keep reminders short and polite. You want the money and the customer.

## Remember that you owe people too

If you buy stock on credit from suppliers, track that as well. A shop can look busy while owing more to suppliers than customers owe it. Knowing both numbers tells you whether you can afford to give more credit.

## How Counterbook tracks credit

[Counterbook](/counterbook/), our free point of sale app for Windows, handles credit inside the same app you sell with.

### Customer accounts with limits

Add a customer with a name and an optional phone, email and credit limit. When you take payment, choose **Credit** and the amount goes on that customer's account. Counterbook respects the limit, so a credit sale that would take them past it is refused.

Anyone past their limit is flagged **Over limit** on the Customers tab.

### Balances at a glance

The Customers tab shows two totals: **Owed to you** and **You owe**. Open a customer to see their history, record a payment or adjust their balance. Customers can also hold store credit.

Supplier balances work the same way. When you receive goods you have not paid for, the supplier's balance goes up, and you record payments from the supplier's page.

### Invoices with due dates

Turn on invoices in Settings to create quotes and invoices with terms and due dates, record payments against them and share a PDF. Open balances appear in Reports, alongside what customers owe.

### Who can give credit

Each staff member has their own PIN and role. The built-in Cashier role can take credit sales and manage customers, and every action goes into the activity log with their name. You can make your own roles if you want fewer people giving credit.

Keep your follow-up routine going alongside the app. The phone number on each customer's record makes it quick.

The [Counterbook docs](/counterbook/docs/) include a training exercise for staff: sell on credit up to a limit, take a payment, then try a sale over the limit and read the refusal.

## A quick checklist

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Give credit to known regulars | Credit for first-time customers |
| Set a limit per customer | Open-ended "pay when you can" |
| Record every sale and payment | Debts kept in memory |
| Agree on a payment date | "I will pay later" |
| Follow up on the due date | Waiting months before asking |

Credit, handled well, brings back loyal customers. Handled badly, it funds other people's shopping with your stock. Records and limits make the difference. For keeping the cash side tidy too, read [how to stop cash drawer shortages](/blog/stop-cash-drawer-shortages-in-your-shop/).
