Many small shops in Nigeria still run on a notebook, a calculator and memory. That works until you want to know which products make money, how much stock is left, or why the cash at the end of the day does not match what you sold. A point of sale app, or POS app, answers those questions for you.

## POS app or POS terminal?

In Nigeria, "POS" often means the card machine used for withdrawals and transfers. That is a payment terminal. A POS app is different. It is software that records every sale, keeps your stock count and shows you reports. You can use both: the terminal takes the card payment, and the app records that the sale was paid by card.

## What a POS app does

A good POS app replaces the notebook with records you can search and add up. At minimum, it should handle:

- **Sales.** Find an item by tapping, searching or scanning a barcode, take payment and print or share a receipt.
- **Stock.** Every sale reduces the count, every delivery increases it, and you get a warning when something is running low.
- **Customers.** Names, phone numbers and, if you sell on credit, how much each person owes.
- **Cash.** What should be in the drawer at the end of the day, compared with what is actually there.
- **Reports.** Revenue, profit, your best-selling products and your busiest hours.

The real value is in the reports. A notebook tells you what you sold. An app tells you what you earned, which needs the cost of each item as well as its price.

## What to look for in a POS app for a Nigerian shop

Not every POS app suits a small shop here. Check these before you commit:

1. **Does it work offline?** Network and power cuts are part of business. If the app stops when the data runs out, you lose sales.
2. **Does it need an account or subscription?** Some apps charge monthly per till. Add that up over a year before you agree.
3. **Where is your data kept?** On your own device, or on someone else's server?
4. **Can staff have their own logins?** If everyone shares one PIN, you cannot tell who did what.
5. **Does it support split payments?** Customers often pay part cash, part transfer.
6. **Can you back it up?** A stolen laptop should not mean losing years of records.
7. **Does it print on the paper you have?** Most small receipt printers use 58 mm or 80 mm rolls.

## Tips for switching from a notebook

- **Count your stock first.** Your app is only as accurate as the opening count you enter.
- **Enter real cost prices.** Profit reports depend on them.
- **Give each staff member their own PIN.** See [how to stop cash drawer shortages](/blog/stop-cash-drawer-shortages-in-your-shop/) for why this matters.
- **Back up from day one.** A USB drive kept away from the shop is enough to start.
- **Keep the notebook for a week.** Run both side by side until you trust the numbers.
