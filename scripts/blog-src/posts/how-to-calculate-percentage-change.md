Sales went from NGN 400,000 to NGN 460,000. Is that up 15% or up 13%? Prices rose 10% and the new price is NGN 2,750. What was it before?

Most percentage questions in a business fall into a handful of types. Each has one formula, and most mistakes come from dividing by the wrong number.

## A percentage is a number out of 100

15% means 15 out of every 100. To use a percentage in a calculation, divide it by 100. So 15% is 0.15, 7.5% is 0.075 and 120% is 1.2.

Keep that in mind and the rest follows.

## Percent of a number

- **X% of Y** = X / 100 x Y

15% of 200 is 15 / 100 x 200, which is **30**.

You use this for commission, deposits and tax. A sales agent on 5% commission who sells NGN 360,000 of goods earns 5 / 100 x 360,000, which is **NGN 18,000**.

## What percent one number is of another

- **X as a percent of Y** = X / Y x 100

30 out of 200 is 30 / 200 x 100, which is **15%**.

If 45 of your 180 customers last month paid by transfer, that is 45 / 180 x 100, which is **25%**.

## Percentage change

This compares a new value with an old one.

- **Change** = (new minus old) / old x 100

A positive answer is an increase. A negative answer is a decrease.

### Example: sales went up

Last month NGN 400,000, this month NGN 460,000.

1. The difference is 460,000 minus 400,000, which is NGN 60,000.
2. 60,000 divided by 400,000 is 0.15, so sales are **up 15%**.

The 13% figure from the opening comes from dividing by the new value: 60,000 / 460,000 is 13.0%. That answers a different question. Percentage change always divides by the old value.

### Example: a cost went down

A supplier cuts a carton from NGN 12,500 to NGN 11,000.

1. The difference is 11,000 minus 12,500, which is minus NGN 1,500.
2. Minus 1,500 divided by 12,500 is minus 0.12, so the cost is **down 12%**.

## Increase or decrease by a percent

- **Increase by P%** = value x (1 + P / 100)
- **Decrease by P%** = value x (1 minus P / 100)

200 increased by 10% is 200 x 1.10, which is **220**. 200 decreased by 10% is 200 x 0.90, which is **180**.

## Reverse percentages: finding the original

This is the question people get wrong most often. You know the value after a percentage was added or taken off, and you need the value before.

A price went up 10% and is now NGN 2,750. Taking 10% off 2,750 gives NGN 2,475. That is wrong, because the 10% was worked out on the old price, not the new one.

Divide instead:

- **Original after an increase** = new value / (1 + P / 100)
- **Original after a decrease** = new value / (1 minus P / 100)

So 2,750 divided by 1.10 is **NGN 2,500**. Check it: 10% of 2,500 is 250, and 2,500 plus 250 is 2,750.

The same method takes VAT out of a total and finds the price before a discount. See [how to calculate VAT](/blog/how-to-calculate-vat-in-nigeria/) and [how to calculate a discount](/blog/how-to-calculate-a-discount/) for worked examples.

## Common mistakes

### Assuming a rise and a fall cancel out

Going from 50 to 75 is up 50%. Going from 75 back to 50 is down 33.3%, because the second change is measured from 75. The same applies the other way:

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Start | 100 |
| Up 50% | 150 |
| Then down 50% | 75 |

You end 25% below where you began. Each percentage is taken from a different starting value.

### Mixing up percent and percentage points

Your margin goes from 20% to 25%. That is a rise of **5 percentage points**. As a percentage change it is (25 minus 20) / 20 x 100, which is **25%**. Both are correct, but they mean different things, so say which one you mean.

### Adding percentages that should be multiplied

Two price rises of 10% are not a 20% rise. 100 x 1.10 x 1.10 is 121, so the total rise is 21%. Two discounts work the same way in reverse.

### Starting from zero

You cannot work out a percentage change from zero. Going from 0 to 5 customers is not an infinite increase, it is just 5 new customers. Report the actual numbers instead.
