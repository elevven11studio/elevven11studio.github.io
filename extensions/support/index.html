Bug Reports

What to include

None of our extensions sends us diagnostics, so everything we know about a problem is what you tell us. These five lines are usually enough to reproduce one.

  1. Which extension, and its version. The version is at the top of its settings page.
  2. The address of the page it went wrong on, if it is a public one. If it is not, tell us what kind of page it was.
  3. What you expected, and what happened instead.
  4. Your browser and version — Chrome, Edge, Brave and so on. chrome://version has it.
  5. An export, if the report has one. WebInspect can export a whole report as JSON from the popup, which tells us far more than a description can.

Please do not send screenshots containing anything private. A report on your webmail or your bank is not something we need, and not something we want to receive.

In Chrome

Opening, managing and removing

Chrome hides a newly installed extension behind the puzzle-piece button rather than putting it on the toolbar, which is why a fresh install often looks like nothing happened.

Open it

  1. Click the puzzle-piece button to the right of the address bar. Everything installed is listed there.
  2. Click the pin beside the one you want, so its icon sits on the toolbar from then on.
  3. Click that icon to open it. WebInspect also answers Ctrl+Shift+U, WebGuard Ctrl+Shift+W.
  4. Right-clicking a page, an image or selected text offers the same thing from the context menu.

Manage it

  1. Right-click its toolbar icon and choose Options for the extension's own settings: every toggle, every permission explained, and the reset button.
  2. Open chrome://extensions and click Details for Chrome's own controls, including the on/off switch and incognito access.
  3. Site access on that screen is where you widen or withdraw permission. Take it back and the extension says what it can no longer do, rather than failing quietly.
  4. chrome://extensions/shortcuts is where keyboard shortcuts are changed.

Remove it

  1. Right-click its toolbar icon and choose Remove from Chrome, then confirm.
  2. Or open chrome://extensions and press Remove on its card.
  3. That deletes everything it stored on your device along with it. There is no account to close and no server holding a copy, so nothing is left behind and there is nobody to ask.
  4. To clear the data but keep the extension, use its own reset instead: "Reset WebInspect" or "Reset WebGuard" in its settings.

Edge, Brave and Opera install Chrome extensions and work the same way, at edge://extensions, brave://extensions and opera://extensions. The uninstall prompt may offer to report the extension as abusive on the way out; that is Chrome asking, not us, and it is optional. We would rather you told us what went wrong.

Troubleshooting

Common things

The answers below match what the extensions actually do today. If one of them contradicts what you are seeing, that is itself worth reporting.

Any of them

Shared behaviour
The keyboard shortcut does nothing

Chrome hands each shortcut to one extension only, and it refuses to assign one that is already taken, silently. Open chrome://extensions/shortcuts to see what is actually bound and to set your own. Chrome also reserves some combinations for itself, including Ctrl+Shift+I for DevTools, which no extension can claim.

How do I delete everything an extension holds?

Each settings page has a reset that empties its entire local store: "Reset WebInspect" and "Reset WebGuard". Uninstalling removes it all too. Nothing is held on a server, because there is no server, so there is nothing else to request and nobody to ask.

How do I take a permission back?

Where an extension asked for host access after install, the same settings page can withdraw it. You can also manage it in Chrome, under the extension's Details, "Site access". Anything the extension can no longer do, it will say so rather than failing quietly.

Nothing happens on chrome:// pages, the Web Store, or a PDF

Chrome does not allow extensions to read its own pages, the Chrome Web Store, or the built-in PDF viewer. That restriction is the browser's and applies to every extension. The popup will tell you that is what it has run into rather than showing an empty result.

Where is the privacy policy?

Each extension publishes its own, saying exactly what that one reads and stores: WebInspect and WebGuard. There is a link to it inside each settings page too.

WebInspect

Site reports
It will not inspect the page I am on

It is probably on the skip list: 522 well-known sites that WebInspect does not read unless you ask. Large platforms, where a report is not actionable, and pages that routinely hold personal information. The popup names the site and why, and offers both ways out: inspect it this once, or turn "Inspect major and personal sites" on in settings.

There is no score on the toolbar icon

Two settings govern that. "Show the score on the toolbar" puts it there after an inspection, and "Inspect a page when it loads" is what produces one without you asking. The second is off by default and needs access to the pages you visit, which Chrome will ask about separately.

"Check links" is greyed out or does nothing

Link checking is off by default and needs a permission you grant explicitly, because it makes real requests to every link on the page. Turn on "Allow link checking" in settings and grant host access when asked. It only ever runs when you press the button.

A technology it detected is wrong

Detection is rated detected, likely or possible rather than asserted, and every entry shows the evidence behind it. If the evidence looks right but the conclusion is wrong, send us the page address and what it actually runs on: that is exactly the kind of report that improves the detector.

The report is stale after I changed the page

Reports are cached for five minutes, keyed by address, so reopening the popup on the same page is instant. "Clear cached reports" in settings empties it immediately, and the cache holds at most twelve entries in any case.

WebGuard

Phishing and sensitive data
It flagged a site I know is fine

Add it to your trusted sites from the popup and WebGuard stops warning you about it; the list is editable in settings under Trusted sites. Every signal shows what it saw, so you can judge whether the finding is wrong or whether the site is genuinely doing something odd. If it is a real false positive, please send us the address.

Why does it need access to every site?

Because a warning that arrives after you have typed your password is not a warning. Automatic protection means checking each page as it loads, and Chrome asks for that access at install. What is read is listed in full in the privacy policy, and it is sent nowhere. You can turn the individual warnings off in settings if you would rather it stayed quiet.

Threat-list results say "no lists downloaded" or "out of date"

That is the honest answer rather than a clean one: an empty or stale database is never presented as a pass. Lists need "Allow online checks" on, since they have to be downloaded. Some feeds also need a key you supply yourself, because an extension cannot keep a secret; those stay off and say so until you add one.

The password breach check will not run

It needs "Allow online checks" on, and that is off by default. With it off WebGuard makes no network requests at all. With it on, the password is hashed on your device and only the first five characters of the hash are sent, so the check never transmits the password itself.

It warned me about text I pasted, and I want that off

"Warn me before I send sensitive data" in settings turns the paste and blur-time scan off. Worth knowing first: the scan runs inside the page, returns a label, a confidence, an offset and a masked preview, and there is no field in the result for the matched value. It is structurally unable to keep or send what it found.

Where is my history? / I do not want a history

History is off by default. Turned on, a record holds a hostname, a verdict, a score and a time, and pages you merely visit are never recorded. "Clear history" in settings empties it, and turning the setting off stops new records.
